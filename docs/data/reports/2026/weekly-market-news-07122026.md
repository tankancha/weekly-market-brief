## Detailed Event Analysis

### 1. June US Jobs Report — Sharp Downside Miss (Impact Score: 26.25)

**Event Date:** July 2, 2026 (08:30 ET) — at the edge of the window; included because it framed the week's entire rate narrative
**Event Type:** Economic Data
**News Source:** BLS Employment Situation (Tier 1, official); CNBC, Yahoo Finance (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
Nonfarm payrolls rose just **+57,000** versus ~115,000 consensus, and the prior two months were revised **down a combined 74,000** (April to 148k, May to 129k). The unemployment rate ticked **down to 4.2%**, but for the wrong reason — labor-force participation fell 0.3pp to 61.5%, the lowest since March 2021. Leisure/hospitality shed 61,000 jobs; average hourly earnings rose +0.3% m/m (+3.5% y/y). The report is an unambiguous signal that the labor market is cooling.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (Day-of):**
- **Equities:** Read as dovish; tempered near-term hike bets and helped launch the early-week melt-up toward the Dow's record.
- **Bonds:** Front-end supported; futures still price the funds rate drifting toward ~4% by year-end.
- **Commodities:** Modest gold support offset later in the week by hawkish minutes.
- **Currencies:** USD gave back some ground intraday before the week's hawkish repricing.

**Follow-Through:** The soft print anchored a constructive-for-risk backdrop into the July 6 record, even as the Fed minutes later reasserted a hawkish tilt.

**Pattern Comparison:** Expected vs Actual — **Consistent** with the historical "bad news is good news" reaction late in a tightening cycle, though the participation-driven drop in unemployment muddies the read.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Major — a >50% payrolls miss with sizable negative revisions.
**Breadth:** Systemic — rates, equities, FX and commodities all repriced.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — reinforces the labor-cooling trajectory.
**Calculated Score:** (7 × 3) × (1 + 0.25) = **26.25**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
Rate-sensitive growth and small caps benefited most from the dovish read; leisure/hospitality flagged as the weak spot in the internals.

### 2. US–Iran Re-escalation / Strait of Hormuz Strikes (Impact Score: 21.0)

**Event Date:** July 6–11, 2026
**Event Type:** Geopolitical / Commodities
**News Source:** Bloomberg, CNBC, CNN, Al Jazeera (Tier 1/2)

#### Event Summary
After a fragile June memorandum of understanding, Iran struck shipping in the **Strait of Hormuz** — the transit point for roughly **20% of global oil and LNG trade**. Two tankers were hit July 6–7 (a Qatari LNG carrier and a Saudi supertanker). President Trump declared the truce **"over" on July 7**; CENTCOM struck 80+ targets July 7–8, ~140 targets mid-week, and hit again on **July 11** after a further ship attack — while signaling this would not become an open-ended campaign.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (Day-of):**
- **Commodities:** **WTI +~3.5% on the week to ~$71.2; Brent +~5% to ~$76**, with intraday spikes of +4–5%. UAE lifted crude output to a record to offset the disruption, capping the rally.
- **Equities:** Repeatedly shrugged off the risk and closed higher; the Dow gave back part of its record into Friday as Hormuz headlines resurfaced.
- **Currencies/Metals:** Only intermittent safe-haven bid for gold and the dollar.

**Follow-Through:** A persistent but contained risk premium — supply disruption feared more than realized, thanks to spare OPEC+ capacity.

**Pattern Comparison:** Expected vs Actual — **Dampened**. The historical Hormuz playbook implies a +5–15% oil spike and risk-off equities; the muted move reflects ample spare capacity and market skepticism that flows would actually be cut.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Major — a multi-percent, multi-session oil move.
**Breadth:** Cross-Asset — commodities plus equity/FX spillover.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change — collapse of the June truce reopens an escalation path.
**Calculated Score:** (7 × 2) × (1 + 0.5) = **21.0**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
Energy majors and oil-services names firmed with crude; airlines and transports faced margin headwinds from higher jet fuel.

#### Commodity Correlation
Hormuz carries ~20% of seaborne oil; the feared-vs-actual gap kept the premium modest. Historical precedent (2019 Aramco attack: +15% single day) shows how much larger the move can be if flows are genuinely interrupted — the key swing factor is whether spare capacity (UAE, Saudi) can offset. Expected duration: elevated volatility while strikes continue, mean-reverting absent an actual supply cut.

### 4. FOMC June Minutes — Hawkish 9-8 Split (Impact Score: 10.0)

**Event Date:** July 8, 2026
**Event Type:** Monetary Policy
**News Source:** FederalReserve.gov (Tier 1, official); CNBC, Bloomberg (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
Minutes of the June 16–17 meeting — the first under new Chair **Kevin Warsh**, at which rates were held at **3.50–3.75%** — showed officials **split 9-to-8** on a 2026 hike, with "a few" arguing for a June move on sticky inflation. Many participants saw the appropriate year-end rate *above* the current range.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (Day-of):**
- **Bonds:** **Treasury yields rose**; year-end funds pricing pushed toward ~4%.
- **Currencies:** Dollar supported near one-year highs.
- **Commodities:** Gold pressured by higher real-rate expectations.
- **Equities:** Muted response — the rally absorbed the hawkish tone.

**Follow-Through:** Markets price ~25% odds of a hike at the July 28–29 meeting (no SEP) and a hawkish hold as the base case.

**Pattern Comparison:** Expected vs Actual — **Consistent** with a hawkish-hold regime; equities' calm reflects a still-strong earnings backdrop.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Moderate — a rates/FX repricing without an equity shock.
**Breadth:** Cross-Asset — rates, FX and gold.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — cements the hawkish-hold path.
**Calculated Score:** (4 × 2) × (1 + 0.25) = **10.0**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
Yield-sensitive utilities and REITs lagged; the firmer dollar was a mild headwind for multinationals and gold.

## Thematic Synthesis

### Dominant Market Narrative
A resilient, AI-led equity uptrend absorbing two macro shocks — a cooling labor market that eased hike fears, and a Hormuz oil-supply scare that markets judged more feared than realized — while the Fed minutes reasserted a hawkish-hold bias.

### Interconnected Events
The soft jobs print (event 1) and the record equity rally (event 3) were reinforcing on the dovish read; the hawkish minutes (event 4) partly offset that by lifting yields, and the Hormuz premium (event 2) added an inflation/oil crosswind. Net, risk appetite won because none of the shocks was severe enough to break the trend.

### Market Regime Assessment
**Risk Appetite:** Risk-On (cautious)
**Evidence:** Record Dow, S&P +1.8%/Nasdaq +2.1% on the week, VIX 15.84 (−6.3%), small-cap leadership — tempered by firmer yields and a persistent oil risk premium.
**Sector Rotation:** Semiconductors and mega-cap tech led; yield-sensitive utilities/REITs and gold lagged on the hawkish repricing.

### Commodity Deep Dive
- **Energy:** WTI ~$71.2 (+~3.5%), Brent ~$76 (+~5%) — Hormuz-driven, capped by record UAE output and OPEC+ spare capacity.
- **Precious Metals:** Gold ~$4,118, roughly flat-to-lower — Mideast haven bid neutralized by higher real-rate expectations post-minutes.
- **Base Metals:** No acute catalyst; sentiment tracked the constructive risk tone.
- **Agricultural:** Not a market-moving factor this week.

### Anomalies and Surprises
Equities repeatedly closing higher *through* live Hormuz strikes is the week's clearest anomaly — a dampened geopolitical reaction reflecting confidence in spare capacity and a "priced-in" escalation. The unemployment rate falling *despite* a payrolls miss (via lower participation) is a second read-with-care signal.
