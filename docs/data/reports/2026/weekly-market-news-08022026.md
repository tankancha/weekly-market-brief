# Weekly Market News — 08/02/2026

## Executive Summary

Coverage: July 27 – August 2, 2026. Five Tier-1 events defined a week whose headline gain concealed a violent internal rotation. The FOMC held at 3.50%–3.75% on a **9–3 vote with three dissents demanding a hike** — the first three-way unified dissent since September 2016 — and the tape broke: Dow −2.19% (−1,153 points, worst session since April 2025), S&P 500 −1.52%, Nasdaq −1.74%, with the 30Y yield +12bp to 5.21%, a 19-year high. Simultaneously, **more than $1 trillion of global semiconductor and AI-hardware market value was erased** between July 24 and July 29 on peak-capex fear, even as hyperscalers rallied on the very capex that was killing the chipmakers — Azure +43% and AWS +37% powered Microsoft +8% and Amazon +12–15%, while Apple lost over $350B despite an earnings beat. Crude round-tripped from above $100 to $88.36 and back to $90.12. Indices still closed higher — S&P +1.0% to 7,489.72, Nasdaq +1.6% to 25,373.85, Dow +1.0% to 52,485.03 — but July itself was negative for the S&P (−0.1%) and sharply so for the Nasdaq (−3.2%).

## Detailed Event Analysis

### 1. FOMC Holds at 3.50%–3.75% — Three Dissents to Hike (Impact Score: 45.0)

**Event Date:** July 29, 2026, 14:00 ET
**Event Type:** Monetary Policy
**News Source:** FederalReserve.gov, CNBC, Bloomberg (Official / Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
The Committee held the target range at 3.50%–3.75% for a fifth consecutive meeting, by a 9–3 vote. Hammack (Cleveland), Kashkari (Minneapolis) and Logan (Dallas) all dissented in favor of a **25bp increase** — the first time since September 2016 that three policymakers dissented in the same direction. The statement described activity expanding at a "solid pace despite elevated uncertainty that owes, in part, to the conflict in the Middle East," with inflation "elevated" relative to the 2% goal. July carries no SEP; the June 2026 projections had already penciled in one 25bp hike by year-end. Chair Kevin Warsh, in his second press conference, said: "We've missed on inflation for five years and we're going to fix that," while also praising the run-up in bond yields as tightening that might spare the Fed from hiking — an ambiguity Bloomberg headlined as leaving investors "baffled."

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (Day-of, closing):**
- **Equities:** Dow **−1,153.18 (−2.19%)** to 51,594.14, the worst day since April 2025; S&P 500 **−1.52%** to 7,316.15; Nasdaq **−1.74%** to 24,442.94. Industrials −3.42%, Technology −2.36%. Selling accelerated into the close, well beyond the shallower intraday drawdown during the presser.
- **Bonds:** 30Y **+12bp to 5.21%**, a 19-year high; 10Y +7bp to 4.67%; 2Y **−4bp** to 4.236% — a pronounced bear steepener, with 2s10s widening to +45bp from +34bp.
- **Commodities:** Gold **+2%** on the day as the dollar and front-end yields fell.
- **Volatility:** VIX closed at **20.66**, up from 16.64 on July 22.

**Follow-Through:** Equities rebounded hard on Jul 30 (S&P +1.7%, Nasdaq +2.8%), but the **rates repricing stuck** — 10Y at 4.74–4.75% and 30Y at 5.28% by Friday. September hike odds became the single most likely outcome, quoted between 54% (Kalshi) and ~70% depending on venue, with markets pricing two hikes across 2026.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Amplified, with an inverted curve response.** A hold is normally neutral-to-positive for equities. The three-way hawkish dissent converted it into a de facto tightening signal, and the long end — not the front end — bore the adjustment. The 2Y *fell* while the 30Y rose 12bp: this is a term-premium and credibility repricing, not a policy-path repricing.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — Dow −2.19% clears the ±2% severe threshold; the 30Y at a 19-year high is a Severe bond-market event.
**Breadth:** Systemic — equities, the full curve, FX and gold all repriced off one signal.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change — the market has flipped from pricing cuts to pricing one-to-two hikes, inverting the prevailing narrative.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 3.0) × 1.5 = **45.0**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
Industrials (−3.42%) and Technology (−2.36%) led the decline. Long-duration equities — REITs, utilities, unprofitable growth — face the sharpest discount-rate pressure with the 30Y above 5.25%. Financials were relatively insulated by the steepening curve.

### 2. Iran/Hormuz — Crude Round-Trips $100 → $88 → $90 (Impact Score: 37.5)

**Event Date:** July 27–31, 2026
**Event Type:** Geopolitical / Commodity
**News Source:** Reuters, CNBC, Bloomberg (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
The prior week's spike above $100 reversed violently, then partially re-escalated. Monday, Reuters reported (citing a senior Iranian official) that Iran would suspend attacks so long as the US pause in hostilities held; Brent September futures fell **8.7% to $88.36** and WTI **7.5% to $82.61**. Wednesday, Brent recovered above $90 after Trump threatened to "hit Iran hard." Friday, the IRGC said it attacked two tankers transiting the Strait of Hormuz under US naval escort, with four others turning back — yet Hormuz traffic simultaneously recovered from the crisis low of 13 tankers/day against a 33/day norm.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (Day-of):**
- **Commodities:** Brent settled Friday at **$90.12**, WTI at **$84.67**, both up more than 1% on the day but down over 5% on the week. July nonetheless delivered crude's biggest monthly gain since March: **Brent +24%, WTI +21%**.
- **Equities:** Energy was the week's laggard sector despite blowout oil-major profits — ExxonMobil earned $14.5B ($3.48/share, roughly $160M per day) and Chevron $12.1B ($6.11/share) on production +20% y/y.
- **Inflation:** The energy unwind pulled headline June PCE down to 3.7% from 4.1%.

**Follow-Through:** Unresolved. On Sunday Aug 2, OPEC+ agreed in principle to add ~188,000 bpd from September and then **pause quota increases through Q4**, completing the rollback of the 1.65 mb/d cut agreed in 2023 while leaving ~2 mb/d of 2022-vintage cuts in place.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Consistent.** Geopolitical risk premia decay fast on de-escalation headlines and re-inflate on escalation. The market is explicitly *not* pricing full Hormuz closure — which is precisely why the downside was capped at $88 and the level snapped back.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — an 8.7% single-session move is well beyond the ±5% severe threshold for crude.
**Breadth:** Systemic — energy, equities, and the inflation path the Fed is reacting to.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — this was a round trip inside an ongoing conflict, not a resolution. The Strait remains contested.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 3.0) × 1.25 = **37.5**

#### Commodity Correlation
Mechanism remains threat-of-closure premium rather than realized barrel loss. With OPEC+ pausing increases into Q4 and roughly 2 mb/d of cuts still in force, the supply cushion is thin: any genuine Hormuz interdiction would find little spare capacity to absorb it. Separately, Ukraine struck two major Russian refineries on Jul 29 and hit Lukoil's Volgograd plant (~15 mt/yr) on Jul 31, claiming ~42.7% of Russian refining capacity disabled, while the US Senate advanced the Graham sanctions bill 86–12, authorizing tariffs up to 200% on buyers of Russian energy.

### 3. Global Semiconductor / AI-Hardware De-Rating (Impact Score: 22.5)

**Event Date:** July 24–29, 2026
**Event Type:** Corporate / Sector
**News Source:** CNBC, Bloomberg, The Information (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
Between the July 24 close and July 29, **more than $1 trillion of global semiconductor and AI-hardware market value was erased** on fears that AI infrastructure spending is peaking faster than expected. Nvidia shed **$238 billion**, falling 3.5% on its heaviest day and 4.9% on a circular-financing scare. SK Hynix lost **$176B**, closing −9.61% after trading down more than 15% intraday on disappointing earnings and a decision to slow HBM expansion in favor of commodity DRAM. Samsung Electronics lost **$173B** (−5%+), Micron **$113B**, with AMD −6.89% and SoftBank −6.95%. A report that a Chinese state-backed firm had begun mass-producing key chipmaking equipment hit ASML and broadened the selloff. VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) fell over 3% on July 28 alone, a fourth consecutive down day.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate:** The de-rating drove the Nasdaq's six-day losing streak into July 29 and is the single largest contributor to the index's −3.2% July.

**Follow-Through:** Partially reversed. Microsoft's cloud beat triggered a chip-led rebound on July 30, but the sector did not recover its losses.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Inverse.** The defining paradox of the week: **hyperscalers rallied on capex guidance while chipmakers sold off on the same guidance.** Historically, capex commitments from Amazon, Microsoft and Meta are read as direct order flow for semis. That transmission broke — the market treated the guides as evidence of a spending peak rather than a spending ramp, compounded by Meta's "Meta Compute" plan to resell surplus AI capacity, which implies a future supply overhang.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — SK Hynix −9.61% and AMD −6.89% clear the ±5% sector-severe threshold.
**Breadth:** Sector-Wide but globally synchronized — Korea, Japan, Taiwan, the Netherlands and the US.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change — a peak-capex verdict would invalidate the dominant equity thesis of the last three years.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 1.5) × 1.5 = **22.5**

Guidance: Azure +45% cc vs 41.4% consensus. Stock **+8.13%** to $422.30 after hours; **over $600B of market cap added on the week**.

- **Meta (Jul 29):** Revenue **$60.8B, +28%**, a beat; EPS **$6.18 vs ~$7.14**, a ~13.8% miss on legal and severance charges. **Free cash flow collapsed to $784M** as capex hit $31.1B; FY26 capex guidance lifted to **$135–145B** from $125–145B, with soft Q3 revenue guidance. Stock **−9.64%**.

Apple had touched a **$5.0 trillion valuation intraday on July 28**, only the second company ever after Nvidia.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate:** Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet added **nearly $1.5 trillion in combined market value on the week**; Apple and Meta lost capitalization.

**Follow-Through:** Sustained. Friday closed with Amazon leading the Nasdaq to +1.0%.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Inverse to the classic beat-and-raise.** Apple beat both lines with iPhone +22% and fell; Amazon raised capex by $20B and rallied double digits. The market is not paying for the print — it pays for evidence that spend converts to revenue (AWS at 37%, Azure at 43%) and penalizes spend arriving with collapsing free cash flow (Meta at $784M).

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — three of four names moved ±6% to ±15%.
**Breadth:** Sector-Wide — concentrated in tech, with index transmission via weight.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — extends the AI-capex scrutiny regime from the prior week's Alphabet (−7.7%) and Tesla (−14.5%) reactions.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 1.5) × 1.25 = **18.75**

### 5. Q2 GDP +1.5%, PCE 3.7%, ECI Turns Real Wages Negative (Impact Score: 10.0)

**Event Date:** July 30–31, 2026, 08:30 ET
**Event Type:** Economic Data
**News Source:** BEA.gov, BLS.gov, Conference Board (Official)

#### Event Summary
Real GDP grew at **1.5% annualized** in Q2, below the 1.8% consensus and down from 2.1% in Q1, on a downturn in government spending. June PCE showed headline **−0.1% m/m, 3.7% y/y** (from 4.1%) with core **+0.1% m/m, 3.3% y/y** — a fourth straight month at or above 3.3%. The Employment Cost Index rose **0.9% q/q and 3.4% y/y**; set against 3.7% PCE inflation, that means **real annual wage growth turned negative at −0.4%, the first loss of worker purchasing power since 2022**. Consumer confidence fell to **90.8** vs 92.3 expected, with the labor differential at its weakest since February 2021. Offsetting positives: jobless claims 197,000 with the 4-week average at a two-year low of 202,650, Chicago PMI 57.6, and UMich sentiment at a five-month high of 55.2 — though 1-year inflation expectations held at **4.2%**.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate:** Offsetting. The growth miss argued for easing; sticky core and a 3.4% ECI validated the hold delivered the day before. Yields continued higher into month-end.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Dampened.** A 30bp GDP miss would normally bid duration; it did not, because the headline PCE improvement was visibly energy-driven and therefore reversible with crude.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Moderate — no single-session index move above ~1% was attributable to the data.
**Breadth:** Cross-Asset — equities and fixed income.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — 1.5% growth with 3.3% core and negative real wages is the stagflation-lite mix the three dissenters are reacting to.
**Calculated Score:** (4 × 2.0) × 1.25 = **10.0**

## Thematic Synthesis

### Dominant Market Narrative
A stagflationary policy squeeze. Growth undershot at 1.5% while core inflation sits at 3.3% after five years above target; the Fed held but with its most hawkish dissent bloc since 2016; and the bond market responded by **steepening violently rather than rallying** — the 30Y at a 19-year high is the market questioning whether the Fed is behind the curve. Equities still closed up ~1% because the AI cloud trade re-validated itself, even as the semiconductor complex lost over $1 trillion on the fear that the same capex cycle is peaking.

### Interconnected Events
Strongly **offsetting**. The crude round-trip (#2) delivered the headline PCE improvement (#5) that partially cushioned the hawkish hold (#1). The earnings bifurcation (#4) and the semi de-rating (#3) are two readings of one fact — hyperscaler capex — resolved in opposite directions. Attribution caution: Friday's advance coincided with month-end rebalancing, so a portion is flow rather than news.

### Market Regime Assessment
**Risk Appetite:** Mixed — index gains masking deteriorating internals.
**Evidence:** S&P +1.0% and Nasdaq +1.6% on the week, but the index rose while the average stock fell; July closed negative for the S&P (−0.1%) and Nasdaq (−3.2%) against the Dow's fourth straight winning month (+0.3%); VIX spiked to 20.66 before settling near 16; the 30Y at 5.28%. Credit stayed benign — US high-yield OAS widened only to 284bp from 269bp, and IG at 80bp remains historically tight, which argues this was an equity-positioning event, not a credit event.
**Sector Rotation:** Into hyperscalers and Communication Services; violently out of semiconductors and AI hardware; out of Energy despite record oil-major profits as Brent fell; Materials ~−2%.

### Commodity and FX Deep Dive
- **Energy:** Brent $90.12, WTI $84.67 — down over 5% on the week but +24%/+21% in July. OPEC+ pauses hikes after September.
- **Precious Metals:** Gold ended near **$4,043–4,062** after rising 2% on FOMC day and briefly losing $4,000 mid-week (low $3,995.90). Weekly direction is source-conflicted (+0.5% Kitco vs −0.22% Forbes); July was gold's first monthly gain in five. It remains ~28% below its January 29 record of $5,597.23. Silver −2.35% Friday to ~$57.6.
- **Base Metals:** Copper $5.99/lb, its lowest since June, though up ~2% on the week.
- **Currencies:** The dollar **weakened** despite hawkish rates — DXY closed July at **99.83, −1.3%**, its worst month since April. The standout was the **yen surging over 3% on Thursday, its largest single-day gain since 2022**, on suspected large-scale MOF/BOJ intervention during US hours, from a 40-year low of 164 to 157.40. EUR/USD closed above resistance at 1.1518.

### Anomalies and Surprises
1. **A hold produced the worst day since April 2025.** The dissent composition, not the decision, was the market-moving variable.
2. **The curve bear-steepened on a hawkish Fed** — 2Y −4bp while 30Y +12bp. Hawkish policy normally lifts the front end; the long end moving instead points to term premium and credibility, not policy path.
3. **Hyperscalers and their suppliers moved in opposite directions on identical news.** A $1T semi drawdown alongside a $1.5T hyperscaler gain in the same week is a correlation breakdown with no recent precedent.
4. **The dollar fell 1.3% in a month the Fed turned hawkish** — explained by Japanese intervention, not US fundamentals.
5. **Apple touched $5 trillion on Tuesday and lost $350B by Friday**, on an earnings beat.

## Forward-Looking Implications

### Market Positioning Insights
The index-up/average-stock-down divergence and the $1T semi drawdown say the rally is narrow and concentrated in three names. Equity multiples now carry a rates assumption that flipped sign inside two weeks — from cuts to a 54–70% probability of a September hike — while the 30Y sits at a 19-year high. Benign credit spreads (HY 284bp, IG 80bp) are the main argument that this is a rotation rather than the start of a de-risking cycle. With negative real wages and consumer confidence at 90.8, the consumer is the weak link the data has not yet fully priced.

### Upcoming Catalysts
- **ISM Manufacturing PMI (July)** — Monday, August 3
- **July jobs report (NFP)** — Friday, August 7 (June was +57K with 74K of downward revisions)
- **July CPI** — Wednesday, August 12
- **Nvidia FQ2 earnings** — late August, the decisive AI-capex verdict after a $238B drawdown

### Risk Scenarios
1. **September hike delivered into 1.5% growth** — Probability ~60%, Impact Major. Now the market's base case. Tightening into decelerating growth and negative real wages is the classic policy-error setup.
2. **AI capex cycle confirmed as peaking** — Probability ~35%, Impact Severe. The semis are already voting. Nvidia's August print either refutes the $1T de-rating or ratifies it.
3. **Hormuz interdiction escalates to sustained closure** — Probability ~30%, Impact Severe. With OPEC+ pausing increases and ~2 mb/d of cuts still in force, spare capacity is too thin to absorb a genuine supply loss; crude would reverse the PCE improvement and force the Fed's hand.
4. **Long-end disorder** — Probability ~25%, Impact Major. A 30Y at a 19-year high with the curve steepening on hawkish news is a term-premium signal; further steepening would pressure equity valuations independent of Fed policy.
