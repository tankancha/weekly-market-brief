# Weekly Market News — 08/02/2026

## Executive Summary

Coverage: July 27 – August 2, 2026. Five Tier-1 events drove the tape, and the dominant one was a reversal: the Iran/Hormuz war premium drained out of crude, with Brent collapsing roughly 16% across three sessions from above $100 to ~$84 intraday Tuesday before settling at $90.12 Friday. That disinflationary impulse let equities absorb an openly hawkish FOMC — a 9–3 hold at 3.50%–3.75% with three dissents demanding *higher* rates — and still finish higher: S&P 500 +1.0% to 7,489.72, Nasdaq Composite +1.6% to 25,373.85, Dow +1.0% to 52,485.03. The offsetting force was rates: the 10Y closed July at 4.75% as markets repriced from cuts to one-to-two hikes by year-end. Mega-cap earnings split violently along AI-capex credibility lines — Amazon +10% and Microsoft +8% against Meta −9.6% and Apple −6.7% after hours — with Alphabet, Amazon and Microsoft adding ~$1.5T of combined market value on the week.

## Detailed Event Analysis

### 1. Iran/Hormuz De-escalation — Crude Unwinds the War Premium (Impact Score: 45.0)

**Event Date:** July 27–31, 2026
**Event Type:** Geopolitical / Commodity
**News Source:** CNN, Bloomberg, CBS News (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
The US paused new strikes on Iran for a second consecutive night as talks moved in a positive direction, and Oman–Iran negotiations aimed at restarting shipping through the Strait of Hormuz made visible progress. That sequence directly reversed the prior week's escalation, which had pushed Brent above $100/bbl. The Strait nonetheless remains effectively closed — roughly 13 mb/d of transit has been disrupted — so this was a repricing of *probability*, not a restoration of physical supply.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (Day-of):**
- **Commodities:** Oil −8% Monday (Jul 27), the largest single-day decline since May 25; Brent −~16% across three sessions to ~$84 Tuesday. Friday settles: Brent $90.12, WTI $84.67. WTI traded an $8+ range on the week.
- **Equities:** Energy gave back leadership; broad indices rallied on the disinflation read-through.
- **Rates:** Falling crude cut breakevens, but nominal yields rose anyway on Fed repricing.

**Follow-Through:** Partially reversed. Crude retraced from the $84 low back to $90.12 Brent by Friday as traders re-priced the still-shut Strait. July still closed as crude's biggest monthly gain since March (Brent +24%, WTI +21%).

**Pattern Comparison:** **Consistent but amplified.** Geopolitical risk premia historically decay fast once headline escalation pauses; the 16% three-session unwind sits at the violent end of that distribution, reflecting how much speculative length had accumulated above $100.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — a 16% three-session move is triple the ±5% severe threshold for crude.
**Breadth:** Systemic — energy, equities, rates, FX and the inflation path all repriced.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change — headline PCE fell to 3.7% from 4.1% on the energy unwind, altering the inflation trajectory the Fed is reacting to.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 3.0) × 1.5 = **45.0**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
Energy producers surrendered the outperformance earned during the spike; airlines, transports and consumer discretionary were the mirror-image beneficiaries of lower crack spreads and fuel costs.

#### Commodity Correlation
Mechanism: threat-of-closure premium, not realized barrel loss. With no incremental supply actually returning, the floor under crude stayed high — hence the bounce off $84 to $90. Historical precedent (2019 Abqaiq, 2022 Russia) argues risk premia fully decay only when the physical chokepoint reopens; that has not happened, leaving crude asymmetrically exposed to headline reversal.

### 2. FOMC Holds at 3.50%–3.75% — Three Hawkish Dissents (Impact Score: 31.5)

**Event Date:** July 29, 2026, 14:00 ET
**Event Type:** Monetary Policy
**News Source:** FederalReserve.gov, CNBC (Official / Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
The Committee held the target range at 3.50%–3.75% by a 9–3 vote. Cleveland's Hammack, Minneapolis' Kashkari and Dallas' Logan dissented — the first time since September 2016 that three policymakers dissented with a unified view on direction — citing inflation above the 2% target for more than five years. The statement described activity expanding at a solid pace with strong productivity and capital investment, and job gains keeping pace with the workforce. Chair Warsh's press conference did nothing to soften the hawkish read.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (Day-of):**
- **Equities:** Dow −1.5% shortly after 13:45 ET; S&P 500 −0.6%; Nasdaq −0.5%.
- **Bonds:** Yields pressed higher; the 10Y ended the week at 4.75%.
- **Volatility:** VIX spiked to 20.66 on Jul 29 from 16.64 on Jul 22 — above its long-term midpoint.
- **Currencies:** Dollar firm; DXY finished the week at 99.91.

**Follow-Through:** Reversed within 48 hours. VIX collapsed −17.3% to 17.09 by Jul 31, handing back nearly the entire correction premium, as earnings and cheaper crude overrode the policy signal.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Dampened.** A three-dissent hawkish hold would typically sustain multi-day equity pressure. It did not, because the simultaneous crude collapse supplied the disinflation the Fed said it was still waiting for.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Major — Dow −1.5% intraday sits in the ±1–2% major band.
**Breadth:** Systemic — equities, rates, FX and gold all repriced off the same signal.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change — investors now anticipate between one and two rate *hikes* by end-2026, inverting the prevailing easing narrative.
**Calculated Score:** (7 × 3.0) × 1.5 = **31.5**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
Rate-sensitive long-duration assets — REITs, utilities and unprofitable growth — absorbed the worst of the day-of damage. Financials were relatively insulated by the steeper front end.

### 3. Mega-Cap AI-Capex Earnings Bifurcation (Impact Score: 18.75)

**Event Date:** July 29 (post-close) and July 30 (post-close), 2026
**Event Type:** Earnings
**News Source:** CNBC, Yahoo Finance, Reuters (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
Four of the Magnificent 7 reported inside 24 hours, every one of them growing revenue above 10% — and the market's reaction keyed almost entirely on how credibly each defended its AI infrastructure spend.
- **Amazon (Jul 30):** Revenue $200.61B, +20% y/y; AWS +37% to $42.2B, its fastest growth in 18 quarters. FY26 capex raised to $220B from $200B on higher memory costs; Q2 property/equipment spend $54.2B; FCF −$7.6B TTM. Stock **+10%** after hours.
- **Microsoft (Jul 29):** FQ4 revenue $90.01B vs $87.62B consensus, +18%; net income $35.77B, EPS $4.81 (aided by a $3.2B mark on its Anthropic stake). Capex and finance leases +69% to $41B; FY26 capex floor lifted to $130B from $125B. Stock **+8%**.
- **Meta (Jul 29):** Revenue $60.8B, +28%; EPS $6.18, missing consensus by ~13.8% on legal and severance charges. FCF collapsed to $784M as capex hit $31.1B; FY guidance $130–145B with soft Q3 revenue guidance. Stock **−9.64%**.
- **Apple (Jul 30):** EPS $2.02 vs $1.89; revenue $109.42B vs $108.86B; iPhone +22% to $54.3B, its strongest quarter in years. Guided next quarter to 9–11% growth vs 12% expected, citing supply constraints. Stock **−6.65%** after hours to $311.25 from a $333.43 close.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (Day-of):** Microsoft's cloud beat triggered a chip-led rebound that snapped the Nasdaq's six-day losing streak. Alphabet, Amazon and Microsoft added nearly $1.5 trillion in combined market value on the week; Apple and Meta lost capitalization.

**Follow-Through:** Sustained. The Nasdaq closed Friday +1.0% at 25,373.85 with Amazon leading, capping a +1.6% week — even as July finished −3.2% for the index.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Inverse to the classic beat-and-raise pattern.** Apple beat on both lines with 22% iPhone growth and still fell nearly 7%; Amazon raised capex by $20B and rallied 10%. The market is no longer paying for the print — it is paying for evidence that spend converts to revenue (AWS at 37%) and penalizing spend that arrives with deteriorating free cash flow (Meta at $784M).

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — three of four names moved ±6.65% to ±10%, above the ±10%/±5–10% mega-cap thresholds.
**Breadth:** Sector-Wide — concentrated in tech and semis, with index-level transmission via weight.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — extends the AI-capex scrutiny regime that dominated the prior week's Tesla and Alphabet reactions.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 1.5) × 1.25 = **18.75**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
Semiconductors and AI infrastructure suppliers rallied on the capex guides — Amazon's $220B and the Microsoft/Meta $130–145B ranges are direct order flow. Ad-dependent internet names de-rated on Meta's guidance. Hardware/consumer electronics softened on Apple's supply-constrained outlook.

### 4. Q2 GDP Advance +1.5% and June PCE Cools to 3.7% (Impact Score: 10.0)

**Event Date:** July 30, 2026, 08:30 ET
**Event Type:** Economic Data
**News Source:** BEA.gov (Official)

#### Event Summary
Real GDP grew at a 1.5% annualized rate in Q2, below the 1.8% WSJ consensus and down from 2.1% in Q1. The deceleration reflected a downturn in government spending plus slower investment and exports, partly offset by accelerating consumer spending. The same morning, June PCE showed headline prices −0.1% m/m, dropping the annual rate to 3.7% from 4.1%, while core rose 0.1% m/m and held at 3.3% y/y — the fourth straight month at or above 3.3%, the longest such stretch since autumn 2023.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (Day-of):** The combination cut both ways and largely offset: a growth miss argued for easing, but core stability at 3.3% validated the Fed's hold delivered the day before. Yields continued higher into month-end.

**Follow-Through:** Consolidated. Equities looked through the data to earnings.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Dampened.** A 30bp GDP miss would normally bid duration; it did not, because the headline PCE improvement was visibly energy-driven and therefore reversible with crude.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Moderate — no single-session index move exceeded ~1% attributable to the data.
**Breadth:** Cross-Asset — equities and fixed income.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — sub-2% growth with sticky 3.3% core is the stagflation-lite mix the three FOMC dissenters are reacting to.
**Calculated Score:** (4 × 2.0) × 1.25 = **10.0**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
Defensives and quality balance sheets are favored by decelerating growth; cyclicals face a narrowing runway if core stays pinned above 3%.

### 5. 10Y to 4.75%, Gold Slides as Hike Odds Rebuild (Impact Score: 8.0)

**Event Date:** July 31, 2026
**Event Type:** Rates / Commodity
**News Source:** ETF Trends, FXStreet, Trading Economics (Tier 1 / Tier 2)

#### Event Summary
The 10Y Treasury finished July at 4.75%, extending the prior week's push to multi-month highs as the market priced one-to-two hikes by year-end. Gold fell 1.47% to $4,042.97, unable to reach $4,100, with the DXY effectively flat at 99.91 (−0.05%).

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (Day-of):** Gold's decline tracked the yield surge almost tick-for-tick — a real-rate story, not a safe-haven story. Equities rallied regardless, with Amazon-led tech absorbing the higher discount rate.

**Follow-Through:** Sustained into month-end.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Consistent.** Rising nominal and real yields raise the opportunity cost of a non-yielding asset; gold behaved exactly as the framework predicts.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Moderate — gold −1.47% sits in the ±0.5–1.5% moderate band; the 10Y move was ~5bps on the week.
**Breadth:** Cross-Asset — rates, precious metals, FX.
**Forward Significance:** Isolated — a second-order consequence of the FOMC repricing rather than an independent driver.
**Calculated Score:** (4 × 2.0) × 1.0 = **8.0**

#### Commodity Correlation
Notably, gold did *not* bid on the Middle East de-escalation reversing — geopolitical hedging demand is being overwhelmed by the real-rate channel. That correlation breakdown is the cleanest tell that rates, not war, now set the marginal price of gold.

## Thematic Synthesis

### Dominant Market Narrative
Two hawkish forces — a three-dissent FOMC and a 4.75% 10Y — were neutralized by one disinflationary one: crude giving back its war premium. Equities finished up on the week not because policy improved, but because the energy shock that was driving the inflation problem partially unwound.

### Interconnected Events
Strongly **offsetting**, with a clear dominant factor. The oil collapse (#1) mechanically produced the headline PCE improvement (#4) that let markets tolerate the hawkish hold (#2). Simultaneously, the earnings bifurcation (#3) supplied idiosyncratic upside independent of macro. The rate/gold move (#5) is a derivative of #2. Attribution caution: Friday's +0.7% S&P advance coincided with month-end rebalancing, so a portion is flow rather than news.

### Market Regime Assessment
**Risk Appetite:** Mixed — index gains masking deteriorating internals.
**Evidence:** S&P +1.0% and Nasdaq +1.6% on the week, but the index rose while the average stock fell; VIX round-tripped 16.64 → 20.66 → 17.09; the 10Y at a multi-month 4.75% high; July itself closed negative for the S&P (−0.1%) and sharply so for the Nasdaq (−3.2%) despite a fourth straight winning month for the Dow (+0.3%).
**Sector Rotation:** Out of energy as crude unwound; back into mega-cap cloud and semis on capex-justification winners; away from ad-tech and consumer hardware; rate-sensitive defensives pressured by 4.75%.

### Commodity Deep Dive
- **Energy:** Brent settled $90.12, WTI $84.67 on Jul 31 — down roughly 7–8% on the week from ~$97/~$92, yet July still delivered the biggest monthly gain since March (Brent +24%, WTI +21%). Hormuz remains shut; ~13 mb/d of transit disrupted.
- **Precious Metals:** Gold $4,042.97, −1.47% Friday and roughly flat on the week, capped below $4,100 by the yield surge.
- **Base Metals:** No Tier-1 dislocation this window; copper remains the cleanest read on whether 1.5% GDP is a soft patch or a trend.

### Anomalies and Surprises
1. **Apple beat and fell 6.65%** with iPhone revenue +22%. Guidance and margin trajectory now dominate the print entirely.
2. **Amazon raised capex $20B and rallied 10%** — the inverse of Meta, which raised capex and fell 9.6%. The differentiator was AWS at 37% growth versus Meta's $784M free cash flow.
3. **Gold ignored geopolitical de-escalation.** With safe-haven demand fading and real rates rising, both channels pointed lower and gold still only lost ~1.5%.
4. **A three-dissent hawkish hold produced a positive week.** Historically an unusual outcome, explained almost entirely by the crude unwind.

## Forward-Looking Implications

### Market Positioning Insights
The index-up/average-stock-down divergence flagged on Jul 31 argues the rally is narrow and capex-winner-concentrated, not broad. With the market now pricing one-to-two hikes rather than cuts, equity multiples are carrying a rates assumption that has flipped sign inside two weeks — leaving valuations dependent on crude staying contained. VIX at 17.09 with 20-day realized volatility at 11.9% and falling means downside protection is inexpensive relative to the headline risk still embedded in an unopened Strait of Hormuz.

### Upcoming Catalysts
- **July jobs report (NFP)** — Friday, August 7
- **July CPI** — Wednesday, August 12
- **Nvidia FQ2 earnings** — late August, the final and largest AI-capex verdict
- **Hormuz reopening talks (Iran–Oman)** — ongoing, headline-driven

### Risk Scenarios
1. **Hormuz talks collapse and crude re-spikes above $100** — Probability ~35%, Impact Severe. Would simultaneously reverse the PCE improvement and validate the three FOMC dissenters, forcing a hike into slowing 1.5% growth.
2. **Core PCE breaks above 3.3% in the July print** — Probability ~30%, Impact Major. A fifth month of stalled core disinflation converts the hawkish hold into an actual hike and re-prices the entire curve.
3. **AI-capex funding stress** — Probability ~25%, Impact Major. Amazon at −$7.6B FCF, Meta at $784M and aggregate 2026 guides near $500B across the complex mean any credit-market tightening at 4.75% hits the capex thesis directly.
4. **Growth undershoot confirmed by August payrolls** — Probability ~25%, Impact Moderate. Following 1.5% Q2 GDP and a +57K June print, a weak July NFP shifts the debate from hikes to hard landing.
