# Weekly Market News — 08/09/2026

## Executive Summary

Coverage: August 3 – 9, 2026. Two shocks defined the week and both cut the same way. On Monday, **Trump called off planned strikes on Iran** conditioned on a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and by Wednesday Iran had agreed proposed shipping routes with Oman — crude shed **−7.8% (Brent) and −7.6% (WTI)** on the week. On Friday, **July payrolls contracted 23,000 against +83,000 consensus**, with 103,000 of prior gains revised away and wages at **+3.2% y/y**, the slowest since May 2021; September hike odds collapsed from roughly **67% a week earlier to 40–44%**. The S&P 500 closed at a **record 7,757.64, +3.58% and its best week since April**; the semiconductor complex took back the prior week's $1 trillion de-rating with **Nvidia +11.6%**, its largest weekly market-cap gain in over a year. VIX was crushed to **14.90**. The hawkish case did not die quietly — ISM Manufacturing printed a four-year high and Kashkari publicly called for a September hike — it was simply overwritten in a single 08:30 print.

## Detailed Event Analysis

### 1. July Employment Report — Payrolls Contract (Impact Score: 31.5)

**Event Date:** August 7, 2026, 08:30 ET
**Event Type:** Economic Data
**News Source:** BLS.gov, CNBC, Reuters (Official / Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
Nonfarm payrolls **fell 23,000** against a **+83,000** consensus — a ~106k miss, and the first outright decline in months. Government shed **53,000**; private payrolls added just **30,000** against +78,000 expected, with weakness in retail and leisure/hospitality and sub-trend healthcare. **May and June were revised down a combined 103,000.** The unemployment rate *fell* to **4.1%** from 4.2%, but only because the labour force contracted **264,000** — participation dropped to **61.4%, the lowest since early 2021**. Average hourly earnings were near-flat m/m and **+3.2% y/y, the slowest since May 2021**.

The week had telegraphed it: **ADP private payrolls came in at +44,000 on Wednesday against +70,000 expected**, the weakest of the year, with June revised to +95K.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (Day-of, closing):**
- **Equities:** S&P 500 **+0.62% to 7,757.64**, a record close; Nasdaq Composite **+1.3% to 26,690.62**; Dow **+151.83 (+0.28%) to 54,036.93**; Russell 2000 **+1.10% to 3,034.49**.
- **Bonds:** 10Y **−7bp on the day to 4.639%**; 2Y to **4.193%**; 30Y **5.192%**.
- **Currencies:** DXY to a **two-week low**, closing a week in which it fell ~**1.0%** — its worst in three months. EUR/USD reached a seven-week high near **$1.156**.
- **Commodities:** Gold **+2.4% to +2.5%** on the day to **$4,347–4,353**.
- **Volatility:** VIX **14.90**, versus 20.66 a week earlier.

**Follow-Through:** September hike odds fell to **40–44%** (hold odds 45% Thursday → 60% Friday) from ~57% pre-print and ~67% a week earlier — roughly a **25pp collapse in five sessions**. No fade into the close.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Amplified, and inverted versus the classic template.** A payroll contraction normally reads as recessionary and pressures equities. Here it was received as pure policy relief: with three FOMC members having dissented in July in favour of a *hike*, a labour-market crack is the only thing that credibly disarms them. Equities rallied on bad news because the discount rate, not earnings, is the binding constraint — and with S&P 500 blended Q2 EPS growth running at **+50.4% y/y** (+32.0% excluding Alphabet and Amazon, per FactSet), the earnings leg needed no help.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Major — index moves were sub-1%, but the repricing was violent: ~17pp shift in September odds on the day, an 11bp weekly rally in the 10Y, the dollar's worst week in three months.
**Breadth:** Systemic — equities, the curve, FX and precious metals all repriced off one release.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change — reverses the hawkish repricing that defined the prior week and returns September to a genuine coin-flip.
**Calculated Score:** (7 × 3.0) × 1.5 = **31.5**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
The **front end led and the long end barely moved**: 2s10s finished near **+44.6bp**, only marginally flatter. That matters — the collapse in hike odds did *not* repair the term-premium problem that took the 30Y to a 19-year high in late July. The 30Y ended at 5.192%, essentially where it started the week. Long-duration equities captured the relief; financials lagged on the front-end-led flattening.

### 2. Hormuz De-Escalation — Crude Sheds 7.8% (Impact Score: 25.0)

**Event Date:** August 3–5, 2026
**Event Type:** Geopolitical / Commodity
**News Source:** Reuters, CNN, NPR, CNBC (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
**Monday Aug 3:** Trump announced he was holding off on planned strikes against Iran — reportedly including strikes on the power grid — at the urging of regional allies, *"subject to being able to rapidly make a DEAL"* to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. **Wednesday Aug 5:** Iran announced it had reached agreement with **Oman** on proposed Hormuz shipping routes after more than three weeks of talks, covering inbound/outbound routing, security arrangements and a proposed **joint coordination centre**; Treasury Secretary Bessent said a deal could land *"today or tomorrow."* **By Friday and into the weekend the terms surfaced, and they were not the ones Washington wanted:** Iran says the agreement **bars US and Israeli vessels**, claims responsibility for navigation and maritime security, and conditions free transit on the US ending its blockade of Iranian ports.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (Aug 3):**
- **Commodities:** **WTI −5.2% to $80.25; Brent −4.7% to $83.75** — a three-week low.
- **Equities:** S&P 500 **+1.48% to 7,600.50**; Nasdaq **+2.1%**; Dow **+~1.4% (~700 points) to a record close**. Amazon topped a **$3 trillion** market cap for the first time.

**Follow-Through:** Oil bounced Thursday as the deal stalled, dragging equities (Dow −0.9%) and lifting yields. Friday settled **WTI ~$78.20, Brent ~$83.0–83.55**. **Weekly: Brent −7.8%, WTI −7.6%.**

**Pattern Comparison:** **Consistent.** A credible de-escalation headline into a market carrying a large geopolitical risk premium produces exactly this — a violent one-day liquidation, then a range while the market waits for terms.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — −7.8% on the week clears the ±5% severe threshold for crude.
**Breadth:** Cross-Asset — energy, equities, breakevens and the policy path.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — extends the round-trip from above $100 that began in late July.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 2.0) × 1.25 = **25.0**

#### Commodity Correlation
Energy is the transmission channel that matters most. Crude near $83 versus above $100 in mid-July removes most of the headline-CPI impulse the FOMC hawks cited in their July dissents — and it is what made Friday's dovish repricing *stick* rather than fade. Precedent from 2018-Q4 and 2022-H2 suggests a 15–20% sustained decline in crude takes 40–60bp off headline CPI with a one-to-two-month lag. **The asymmetry is unattractive:** the market has priced a de-escalation discount on an agreement the US has not accepted. Supply risk elsewhere is also live — a Ukrainian drone strike on Thursday set fire to Russia's **Slavneft-Yanos refinery (~300,000 bpd)** at Yaroslavl. Note also that OPEC+'s August 2 decision *paused* quota increases through Q4 after a ~188,000 bpd September addition; the group was not the source of this week's weakness.

### 3. Semiconductor Complex Round-Trips the $1T De-Rating (Impact Score: 22.5)

**Event Date:** August 3–7, 2026
**Event Type:** Corporate / Sector
**News Source:** CNBC, Bloomberg, Reuters, UBS (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
The peak-capex panic that erased more than **$1 trillion** of global semiconductor and AI-hardware value between July 24 and 29 reversed almost entirely. **Nvidia rose 11.6% on the week** — its largest weekly market-cap gain in over a year — to a **$5.43 trillion** capitalisation, still trading below its record ahead of August 26 earnings. Three catalysts: **UBS raised DRAM contract price forecasts** to **+32% for Q3 (from +17%)** and **+18% for Q4 (from +12%)**; the **SpaceX–Nvidia "Starmind AI1"** orbital data-centre partnership was announced Tuesday, built on Vera Rubin silicon with a 2027 launch target; and AMD's data-centre print supplied fundamental confirmation. SK Hynix and Samsung rallied hard in Seoul.

#### Market Reaction
- **Equities:** Nasdaq **+5.19%** versus S&P **+3.58%** versus Dow **+2.96%** — a 223bp growth-over-Dow spread and a textbook duration/beta ordering. SMH ended around **$571–583**, up **61.87% YTD**.
- **Volatility:** VIX **20.66 → 14.90**, a ~28% collapse.

**Follow-Through:** Sustained into Friday's record close with no distribution.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Amplified.** The template for a capex scare is a multi-week base before recovery. This was a five-session V, which argues the de-rating was positioning-driven rather than a genuine revision to the AI capex trajectory.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — NVDA +11.6% in a $5T name is a systemic-weight move.
**Breadth:** Sector-Wide — concentrated in semis and AI hardware, though large enough in index weight to drive the Nasdaq.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — reasserts the AI capex trade after the sharpest challenge to it this year.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 1.5) × 1.5 = **22.5**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
The hyperscaler-versus-chipmaker bifurcation of the prior week — clouds up, silicon down — closed; both legs trade on the same capex signal again. The counterweight came from **SpaceX's first public quarter** (Tuesday): revenue **$7.81B, +92%**, adjusted EBITDA **$3.54B, +191%** — against **~$18.4B of quarterly capex, more than 2x revenue**. The stock sat **>50% below its June 16 peak of $225.64 and ~20% below its $135 IPO price** by Wednesday. Thursday's lockup expiry released **911.5M shares**, taking public float from **4.9% to 11.8%**, and the stock still closed **+6.1%**. It passed the supply test and failed the capex test — the cleanest available market verdict on AI spending without near-term returns.

### 5. The Hawkish Case That Lost — ISM 55.6 and Kashkari's Hike Call (Impact Score: 10.0)

**Event Date:** August 3 and August 5, 2026
**Event Type:** Economic Data / Fed Communication
**News Source:** ISM, CNBC (Official / Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
**ISM Manufacturing (Aug 3): 55.6**, +2.3pp and the **highest since May 2022**; New Orders 56.7 and **Employment 52.8 — the first expansion in 33 months**. **ISM Services (Aug 5): 54.1**, a 25th consecutive expansionary month, with Business Activity 59.1 and New Orders 57.2 — but **Employment 47.4** back in contraction and **Prices Paid at a hot 70.3**. On Wednesday, **Kashkari** — one of the three July dissenters — said at the Aspen Ideas Festival: *"I don't see evidence of monetary policy being marginally restrictive right now… I would rather get going now in small steps than wait till later,"* explicitly favouring a start as early as September.

#### Market Reaction
This combination held September hike odds near **55–67% through Thursday** and drove the week's only real risk-off session: **Dow −0.9% (−450+ points) on Aug 6** with yields higher. Friday's payrolls overwrote it entirely. The hawkish case was postponed, not refuted.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Moderate — one −0.9% Dow session, no durable curve move.
**Breadth:** Cross-Asset — informs both the growth and policy debates.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — activity surveys remain firmly expansionary.

## Thematic Synthesis

### Dominant Market Narrative
**The hawkish repricing was unwound in five sessions, but only at the front end.** Last week closed with the market pricing one-to-two 2026 hikes and the 30Y at a 19-year high. This week September is a coin-flip, the 2Y is at 4.193%, and the S&P is at a record — yet the **30Y finished at 5.192%, essentially unchanged**. The market has repriced the *policy path* without repricing the *term premium*. That distinction is the most important thing in this report.

### Interconnected Events
**Reinforcing, and sequenced.** The Hormuz de-escalation on Monday removed the energy-inflation argument; the payroll contraction on Friday removed the wage-inflation argument. The semiconductor recovery is downstream of both — long-duration equities are the highest-beta expression of falling discount rates — with Caterpillar's $72B backlog and AMD's data-centre growth supplying the fundamental justification. The ISM beats and Kashkari's hike call are the dissonant note, and the reason this is a **policy-relief rally rather than a growth-scare rally**.

### Market Regime Assessment
**Risk Appetite:** **Risk-On**, and unusually clean.
**Evidence:** Nasdaq **+5.19%** > S&P **+3.58%** > Dow **+2.96%** — textbook duration/beta ordering. VIX **20.66 → 14.90**. **US HY OAS tightened to ~271bp from ~284bp** (IG ~81bp, BBB ~100bp) — among the richest decile in its history against a long-run median near 450bp. No safe-haven bid anywhere.
**Sector Rotation:** Into growth, high-beta and large-cap tech; out of **energy, commodities and REITs**. Financials lagged on front-end-led flattening.

### Commodity Deep Dive
- **Energy:** Brent ~**$83** (−7.8% wk), WTI ~**$78.20** (−7.6% wk), driven entirely by Hormuz de-escalation. **US natural gas −2% to a 14-week low** (~$2.66/MMBtu) on a +33 Bcf build.
- **Precious Metals:** Gold **$4,315–4,353** (sources differ), **+7% to +7.5% — its best week since January**. Silver **+10.23% to $64.10**. **This is a seven-week high, not a record:** gold remains roughly **22% below its January 29 record of $5,597.23**. The move is a real-rate and dollar trade, not a haven trade — it rose alongside equities with VIX falling.
- **Base Metals:** Copper **+1.07%**, with LME and SHFE inventories both drawing down. Still the cleanest read on whether ISM or payrolls is telling the truth.

### Anomalies and Surprises
1. **A payroll contraction produced a record equity close.** Bad news is good news while three FOMC members are dissenting toward a hike.
2. **ISM Manufacturing employment expanded for the first time in 33 months in the same month payrolls went negative.** Survey and hard data are in open contradiction; one loses by September.
3. **Initial claims came in at 199,000 — a third straight week below 200,000, the longest such streak since 1969** — alongside Challenger job cuts at a two-year low of 33,429. A labour market that is not firing but has stopped hiring. Unemployment "fell" to 4.1% only because 264,000 people left the labour force.
4. **Hike odds collapsed ~25pp and the 30Y did not move.** Term premium, not policy expectations, now owns the long end.
5. **AMD fell up to 9% on a record quarter with raised guidance.** Positioning and a 200bp gross-margin miss, not demand.

## Forward-Looking Implications

### Market Positioning Insights
The index is at a record on a discount-rate rally layered on a genuinely strong earnings base (+50.4% blended Q2 growth). But VIX at 14.90 and HY at 271bp leave no cushion, and the five-session round-trip in semis cuts both ways — the same positioning fragility that caused the July de-rating can reassert. The single cleanest hedge observation: the market has priced Hormuz relief on terms the US has not accepted.

### Upcoming Catalysts
- **RBA decision · Aug 11** — with quarterly Statement on Monetary Policy.
- **Treasury quarterly refunding auctions · Aug 11–13** — $125B ($58B 3Y, $42B 10Y, $25B 30Y), raising ~$28.7B new cash. The direct test of whether the long end's term-premium problem is still live.
- **July CPI · mid-August** — the print that resolves the September coin-flip.
- **July FOMC minutes · ~Aug 19**, then **Jackson Hole · late August**.
- **Nvidia Q2 earnings · Aug 26** — the final adjudication of the peak-capex debate.

### Risk Scenarios
1. **Hormuz deal collapses on terms (30%, Major):** Iran's version bars US and Israeli vessels and demands the US end its port blockade. Roughly 7–8% of crude downside was priced on an agreement Washington has not signed.
2. **Hot July CPI reverses the week (30%, Severe):** With September at a coin-flip, VIX at 14.90 and ISM Services Prices Paid at 70.3, an upside surprise re-arms the three dissenters from a higher index level.
3. **Payroll weakness proves the leading edge, not noise (25%, Major):** If August confirms contraction, the market pivots from "no hike" to "recession" and the bad-news-is-good-news reflex inverts. Watch copper and HY spreads first.
4. **Long-end supply indigestion (20%, Major):** $125B of refunding into a 30Y that refused to rally even as hike odds collapsed 25pp.
