# Weekly Market News — 09/13/2026

## Executive Summary

Sep 7–11 was a holiday-shortened four-session week in which a naval war repriced the global cost of energy and two central banks moved with it. **WTI settled at $100.05, +9.4% on the week**, its first close above $100 since May, and **Brent at $104.61, +8.7%**, as the US–Iran conflict became an outright tanker war: the US destroyed three Iranian tankers, Iran fired a ballistic missile toward US warships, and **Hormuz transits collapsed to 7 vessels Thursday from 18 on Tuesday**. **US diesel broke $6.00/gal for the first time ever, reaching $6.05 — up roughly 63% year over year.** That shock is already inside the data: **August PPI rose 0.4% m/m and 5.4% y/y**, over three-quarters of it energy, with **diesel alone +24.1% m/m**; **CPI rose 0.4% m/m and 3.4% y/y**. The **ECB hiked 25bp to 2.50% on Sep 10 in a unanimous vote** — Lagarde called it *"a no brainer"* — and September 16 Fed hike odds ran from **62.4% Thursday to roughly 90% Friday**. Five significant events drove the week. Equities fell for four straight sessions before a sharp Friday rally: **S&P 500 7,656.98 (−0.8%)**, **Nasdaq 26,333.04 (−0.7%)**, **Dow 52,573.29 (−1.6%)**, **Russell 2000 −2.4%**, with the **30Y at 5.36%**, its highest since 2007. The complication worth holding onto: **core CPI at 2.4% y/y is the lowest since March 2021**. This is an energy shock, not a broadening inflation.

## Detailed Event Analysis

### 1. The Tanker War and WTI's Return Above $100 (Impact Score: 37.5)

**Event Date:** Sep 7–11, 2026
**Event Type:** Geopolitical / Commodity
**News Source:** CNBC, Al Jazeera, RFE/RL, ABC News, EIA, AAA (Tier 1 + official)

#### Event Summary
The six-month US–Iran war moved decisively to sea. The US destroyed **three Iranian oil tankers** overnight on Sep 9; Iran struck vessels near the Strait of Hormuz and fired a **ballistic missile toward US warships**. CENTCOM's Adm. Brad Cooper framed the doctrine explicitly: *"If you shoot at two of our ships, we will impose an even higher economic cost — taking out three of yours."* Washington added **sanctions on Iran's aviation sector** and is running a **naval blockade of Iranian vessels and ports**. Trump said the US is **not seeking a deal**, suggesting the conflict ends after the November midterms. The distinction that held all summer — military targets, not energy infrastructure — has collapsed, because the tankers now *are* the targets.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (week):**
- **Commodities:** **WTI $100.05, +9.4% w/w** (−2.4% Friday); **Brent $104.61, +8.7% w/w** (−2.8% Friday). Friday's give-back followed Iranian state media confirming **Tehran will meet Gulf states in Oman** to discuss the Strait.
- **Physical energy:** **Hormuz transits fell to 7 vessels Thursday from 18 Tuesday.** **AAA national diesel hit $6.05/gal**, the first print above $6.00 and up from $3.71 a year ago (**≈ +63% y/y**). The EIA's Sep 9 STEO forecasts distillate inventories **below 100mn bbl this month** and below the five-year low **through much of 2027**; Sep 4 stocks stood at 106.3mn bbl.
- **Equities:** Energy is the **best YTD S&P 500 sector at +42%**, with refiners **VLO, PSX, PBF, MPC, DINO and DK at 52-week highs**.

**Follow-Through:** Partially reversed on Friday's diplomacy headline, but the week closed +9%.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Consistent, at last.** For six months the market dampened every Iran headline on the reasoning that the at-risk barrel was already priced out. Destroying tankers removes that cushion, and crude traded like a textbook supply disruption.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — a 9.4% weekly move is nearly double the ±5% severe threshold for crude.
**Breadth:** Systemic — energy, the inflation prints, the policy path and equity sector leadership all trace back to it.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation (+25%) — escalation along an established axis.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 3) × 1.25 = **37.5**

#### Commodity Correlation
Roughly **25% of seaborne oil and 20% of LNG** transited Hormuz pre-war. This remains a risk-premium rebuild rather than a booked loss of productive capacity, which is why one diplomatic headline took 2.4% out of WTI in a session. The distillate picture is the more durable problem: **diesel is where a shipping war, Ukrainian strikes on Russian refining, and sub-five-year-low inventories compound**, and diesel is a direct input to the PPI. **Natural gas at ~$2.80/MMBtu — a three-week low, with a +40 Bcf build against +31 expected and stocks 4.8% above the five-year average — is entirely decoupled.** US gas is a domestic story, not a Hormuz one.

### 2. August CPI and PPI Make September 16 a Live Hike (Impact Score: 31.5)

**Event Date:** Sep 10, 8:30am ET (PPI); Sep 10–11 (CPI)
**Event Type:** Economic Data
**News Source:** BLS, DOL, University of Michigan, CNBC, Bloomberg, CBS News (Official + Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
**CPI rose 0.4% m/m and 3.4% y/y**, both in line with consensus. **Core rose 0.3% m/m against 0.2% expected** — the hot spot — but **core y/y fell to 2.4%, the lowest since March 2021**, continuing a 3.2% → 3.1% → 2.4% descent. **Gasoline +3.9% m/m (+27.4% y/y) supplied more than a third of the monthly increase**; shelter was benign at **+0.3% m/m, +3.0% y/y**. **PPI final demand rose 0.4% m/m and 5.4% y/y**, with goods **+1.1%** against services **+0.1%** — over three-quarters of it **energy (+4.2%)** and over a third of the goods rise **diesel (+24.1% m/m)**. Separately, **University of Michigan preliminary September sentiment collapsed to 47.8 against 51.0 expected**, a second consecutive decline and near May's record low, with **one-year inflation expectations jumping to 4.6% from 4.0% expected**.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate:**
- **Rates:** **30Y 5.36%**, the highest since 2007, after a 7bp jump Thursday to 5.26%; **10Y 4.96–4.98%**; **2Y 4.63%**; **2s10s +35bp**. Thursday's move was amplified by a **weaker-than-expected uptake at the first expanded Treasury buyback operation**.
- **Policy pricing:** CME FedWatch showed **62.4% hike / 37.6% hold / 0% cut on Sep 10**, rising to roughly **90% Friday** and **85.5% on Sep 12**.
- **Equities:** **Higher.** S&P 500 **+0.9%**, Nasdaq **+1.0%**, Dow **+1.0%** on Friday, snapping a four-session losing streak; **nine of eleven sectors rose**, tech, industrials and communication services each above 1%.
- **Volatility:** **VIX 15.84, −11.21%.**
- **Currencies:** **DXY 99.087, +0.04%** — a striking non-reaction.

**Follow-Through:** One up session against four down ones. The rate move is the durable part.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Inverse, and this is the week's central anomaly.** Inflation at 3.4% with hike odds near 90% should sell equities. Instead stocks rallied and the VIX fell 11%. Three partial explanations: the headline *matched* expectations after four sessions of positioning for worse; crude fell 2.4% the same morning, easing the forward path even as the backward-looking print ran hot; and **core at a five-year low gave bulls a legitimate argument that this is an energy shock, not a broadening inflation**. Uncertainty resolution beat the level of the data.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Major — a 30Y at a 19-year high and a ~30-point swing in hike probability, against sub-1% equity moves.
**Breadth:** Systemic — rates, equities, metals and FX.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change (+50%) — the Fed is set to tighten into a supply shock, under a new Chair, with core inflation *falling*.
**Calculated Score:** (7 × 3) × 1.5 = **31.5**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
Rate-sensitives lagged: **health care and utilities** were Friday's weak spots. Labour data gave the Fed no obstacle — **initial claims 206,000**, a four-week average of 206,000, and continued claims of 1,774,000 with a **1.2% insured unemployment rate**. Credit stayed calm: **HY OAS 2.84 and IG 0.80**, both tight historically despite a 30-day widening across rating tiers, with heavy September investment-grade supply absorbed.

### 3. Metals Reverse Off Records (Impact Score: 20.0)

**Event Date:** Sep 10–11, 2026
**Event Type:** Commodity / Policy headline
**News Source:** MINING.COM, Trading Economics, USAGOLD, Rio Times (Tier 1 + specialist)

#### Event Summary
The metals complex broke and the mining-equity rally stopped abruptly — but the driver was **not** primarily the Fed. **Copper fell off a record high** on reports that the **US is reconsidering tariffs on refined copper products**, the same policy channel that drove the metal to its record in the first place. **Silver and gold fell alongside it**, with gold booking a **third consecutive weekly loss** of roughly 1%.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate:** Copper's decline is reported two ways — Comex December down **as much as 5.4% to $6.5160/lb**, and US futures falling to **$6.45/lb from a record $6.50**. Both describe a sharp reversal from a record; the magnitude is unresolved and is left as a range. Gold settled near **$4,348–4,408/oz** depending on contract and venue; silver near **$64.4–64.9**.

**Follow-Through:** Sustained — metals did not join Friday's equity relief rally.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Mixed attribution, and worth stating plainly.** Gold falling for a third straight week during an escalating naval war in the Persian Gulf is a genuine correlation breakdown, and that part is a real-rate story: with the 30Y at a 19-year high, the carry cost of a non-yielding asset is punishing. **Copper is a different animal entirely** — it reversed off a record on a tariff headline, which is a policy event, not a growth signal. Reading a demand warning into this week's copper tape would be a mistake.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — the reversal clears the ±4% base-metals threshold on the higher of the two reported measures.
**Breadth:** Cross-Asset — base metals, precious metals, mining equities.
**Forward Significance:** Isolated Event (0%) — a tariff-policy reversal off a record, not a trend.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 2) × 1.0 = **20.0**

### 5. The ECB Hikes to 2.50%, Unanimously (Impact Score: 12.0)

**Event Date:** Sep 10, 2026
**Event Type:** Monetary Policy
**News Source:** ECB, Bloomberg, FXStreet (Official + Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
The ECB raised the deposit rate **25bp to 2.50%** in a **unanimous** decision — its **second hike since the Iran war began**. Lagarde called the move **"a no brainer"** and said it was **"robust against all three scenarios."** The Bank raised its inflation forecasts and declined to pre-commit; markets responded by pricing **further hikes**.

#### Market Reaction
Expected, and therefore modest on the day — but the signal is the unanimity. A divided Governing Council hiking is a compromise; a unanimous one is a consensus that an energy shock passing through European industry has to be met.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Consistent.** The significance is synchronization: the ECB has hiked twice, the Fed is ~90% priced for Wednesday, and the BoJ is expected to move Friday. The BoE is the outlier — **Bailey told MPs on Sep 8 there is "no secret plan" to raise rates**, with Bank Rate at 3.75% after a 6–3 hold.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Moderate — fully anticipated. **Breadth:** Cross-Asset — European rates and FX. **Forward Significance:** Regime Change (+50%) — synchronized global tightening into a common supply shock.
**Calculated Score:** (4 × 2) × 1.5 = **12.0**

## Thematic Synthesis

### Dominant Market Narrative
Central banks tightening into an energy shock they cannot tighten away. The week's causal chain is unusually clean: Hormuz transits fall by 60% → crude +9% and diesel to a record $6.05 → PPI energy +4.2% and diesel +24.1% → CPI 3.4% → the ECB hikes, the Fed goes to ~90% priced → the 30Y hits a 19-year high. The counter-current that keeps this from being a simple inflation story is **core CPI at 2.4%, the lowest since March 2021**.

### Interconnected Events
Events 1, 2 and 5 are **sequential and reinforcing** — the oil move sits mechanically inside the inflation prints, which is what makes both the ECB hike and the Fed's Wednesday decision defensible. Event 3 is **not** part of this chain, and that is the correction worth making explicitly: copper's reversal was a tariff-policy headline off a record high, and only gold's third weekly decline belongs to the real-rate story. Event 4 is orthogonal to the macro entirely.

### Market Regime Assessment
**Risk Appetite:** Mixed, with a hawkish tilt
**Evidence:** Four consecutive down sessions, the Dow's worst week since March, and the **Russell 2000 −2.4%** — small caps underperforming large by 1.6pp — argue risk-off. A VIX of 15.84, nine of eleven sectors green Friday, and **HY OAS at 2.84** argue otherwise. The stress is in commodities, small caps and the long end, not in large-cap equity or credit.
**Sector Rotation:** Energy leads outright (**+42% YTD**, refiners at 52-week highs). AI and data-centre capex names led Friday. Rate-sensitive defensives lagged. Beneath the index, dispersion was extreme: **Meta +6.55% to $653.69** on the launch of **"Muse,"** an autonomous personal AI agent — the week's best mega-cap move — against **Tesla −5.9%** after NHTSA opened an investigation into whether the **Cybercab** meets federal safety standards. **Apple fell about 1%** on the Sep 9 launch of the **iPhone 18 Pro**, the first foldable **"iPhone Duo,"** and **Siri AI**, the first event under new CEO John Ternus — closely tracking its historical −0.72% announcement-day pattern. **Casey's General Stores fell roughly 16.5%** despite beating on a fuel-margin surge.

### Commodity Deep Dive
- **Energy:** WTI **$100.05 (+9.4% w/w)**, Brent **$104.61 (+8.7% w/w)**, both −2 to −3% Friday. **Diesel $6.05/gal, a record and ≈+63% y/y**, is the most important price in this report. Natural gas **~$2.80/MMBtu** at a three-week low, decoupled.
- **Precious Metals:** Gold **≈$4,348–4,408/oz**, a third straight weekly loss of roughly 1%. Silver **≈$64.4–64.9**. Real rates are beating the war bid.
- **Base Metals:** Copper reversed **off a record high** on the refined-copper tariff headline — a policy event, not a demand signal.

### Anomalies and Surprises
1. **Stocks rallied on 3.4% inflation with a hike ~90% priced** — S&P +0.9%, VIX −11.2%. Position-clearing plus a falling core.
2. **Gold fell for a third week during a naval war in the Persian Gulf.** The safe-haven correlation is broken; real rates own the metal.
3. **The dollar did nothing** — DXY +0.04% through a 30-point swing in hike probability, consistent with a market reading the hike as a *reaction to* a supply shock rather than US strength. Meanwhile the **yen sat near a seven-month high at ~153.4**, pricing the BoJ rather than the Fed.
4. **Consumer sentiment at 47.8** with **one-year inflation expectations at 4.6%** is a recessionary reading that no market priced this week.
5. **Russian drones crossed into Poland and NATO scrambled fighter jets to shoot them down** — the first major spillover beyond Ukraine's borders — and European risk assets barely registered it.

## Forward-Looking Implications

### Market Positioning Insights
Wednesday's hike is close to fully priced, so the risk sits entirely in the **dot plot and Warsh's characterization of a supply-driven print** — his first hike decision as Chair. With core at a five-year low, a hawkish dot plot would be a deliberate choice to lean against energy pass-through rather than look through it. The cleanest expression in equity remains dispersion between *sold* AI capacity and *monetized* AI software. The most underpriced item on the calendar is the collision of a BoJ hike with a record-size quadruple witching.

### Upcoming Catalysts
- **FOMC decision, SEP dot plot and Warsh press conference · Wed Sep 16, 2:00pm ET**
- **August retail sales · Wed Sep 16, 8:30am ET** — prior −0.6% m/m
- **Bank of England · Thu Sep 17** — consensus hold at 3.75% after Bailey's pushback
- **Bank of Japan · Fri Sep 18**, expected to hike to a ~31-year high, into **quadruple witching the same day** — a record-size expiry, the first with CME single-stock futures live

### Risk Scenarios
1. **The Fed hikes and the dots shift higher.** Probability ~40%. Impact **Major** — the hike is priced; a 2027 dot shift is not, and a 30Y at a 19-year high has little cushion.
2. **A Gulf energy facility is struck.** Probability ~20%. Impact **Severe** — vessel attacks support $100 crude; an export terminal hit is the move to $120 and genuine equity risk-off.
3. **BoJ hikes into a record quad witching.** Probability ~35%. Impact **Moderate to Major** — a carry unwind with the yen already at a seven-month high, landing on the largest expiry of the year with neutral-to-negative dealer gamma, is a volatility accident waiting for a catalyst.
4. **The consumer breaks before inflation does.** Probability ~30%. Impact **Major** — sentiment at 47.8, diesel +63% y/y and one-year inflation expectations at 4.6% describe a household sector absorbing a tax it cannot pass on, into a tightening cycle.
5. **NATO–Russia escalation after the Poland incursion.** Probability ~15%. Impact **Severe** — a second, uncorrelated energy shock onto an already-impaired distillate market.
