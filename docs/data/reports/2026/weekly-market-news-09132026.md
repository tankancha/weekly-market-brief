# Weekly Market News — 09/13/2026

## Executive Summary

Sep 7–11 was the week the inflation argument stopped being an argument. Markets were closed Monday for Labor Day, leaving four sessions — and the first three of them were down, extending the losing streak to four straight and giving the Dow its longest run of declines since late April. **August CPI rose 0.4% m/m and 3.4% y/y** with core at **0.3% m/m / 2.4% y/y**, and the day before, **August PPI printed +0.4% m/m and +5.4% y/y** — its final-demand energy component up **4.2%**, with **diesel alone +24.1% m/m**. That is an energy shock arriving in the producer pipeline in real time, because the other half of the week was **WTI settling at $100.05, its first weekly close above $100 since May**, up roughly **9.4%** from last Friday's $91.48, with **Brent at $104.61 (~+9%)**, as the US–Iran conflict became an outright tanker war around the Strait of Hormuz. September 16 hike odds went from roughly 60% at the start of the week to **85–90%** by Friday. Five significant events drove the period. The indices finished lower — **S&P 500 7,656.98 (−0.8%)**, **Nasdaq 26,333.03 (−0.7%)**, **Dow 52,573.29 (−1.6%)** — but the tell was not equities: it was the **metals complex breaking**, with **Comex copper down as much as 5.4% to $6.5160/lb**, **silver −2.6% to $64.90**, and gold grinding to a **third consecutive weekly loss** while the **30Y reached 5.36%**.

## Detailed Event Analysis

### 1. The Tanker War and WTI's Return Above $100 (Impact Score: 37.5)

**Event Date:** Sep 7–11, 2026 (Day 196 of the conflict falls Sep 11)
**Event Type:** Geopolitical / Commodity
**News Source:** Reuters via Yahoo Finance, Al Jazeera, UK Maritime Trade Operations, GlobalSecurity.org (Tier 1 + specialist)

#### Event Summary
The seven-month US–Iran war shifted decisively to sea. The US struck **three Iranian oil tankers**; Iran hit tankers in reply and fired a ballistic missile toward US warships near the Strait of Hormuz, with analysts describing the targeting of two US carriers as a *calculated escalation*. On **Sep 11** the IRGC said it struck an unmanned surface vessel at the strait's entrance, and UKMTO reported **four projectiles striking two vessels four nautical miles west of Khasab, Oman**, one of them afire. The distinction that mattered all summer — military targets, not energy infrastructure — has now eroded: the targets *are* the barrels.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (week):**
- **Commodities:** **WTI $100.05**, up roughly **9.4%** from last Friday's $91.48 and the first weekly close above $100 since May; **Brent $104.61 (~+9%)**. Friday alone gave back **−2.37%** (WTI) and **−2.81%** (Brent) on Hormuz de-escalation headlines.
- **Equities:** The proximate cause of the four-session losing streak; energy was the relative bright spot, the sector climbing about **2.1%** on the crude move.
- **Inflation:** Transmitted directly into the data — **PPI final-demand energy +4.2% m/m, diesel +24.1% m/m**, and CPI gasoline **+3.9% m/m, +27.4% y/y**.

**Follow-Through:** Partially reversed. Friday's 2–3% give-back on diplomatic headlines is the first two-way price action in crude in weeks, but the week still closed +9%.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Consistent, finally.** For six months the market dampened every Iran headline on the logic that the at-risk barrel was already priced out. Direct strikes on tankers removed that cushion, and crude behaved like a textbook supply-disruption event.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — a ~9% weekly move is roughly double the ±5% severe threshold for crude.
**Breadth:** Systemic — energy, the inflation prints, the rates path and equity sector leadership all traced to it.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation (+25%) — escalation of an existing conflict along an established axis, not a new regime.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 3) × 1.25 = **37.5**

#### Commodity Correlation
About **25% of seaborne oil and 20% of LNG** transited Hormuz before the war. The move is a risk-premium rebuild rather than a booked loss — no export terminal has been destroyed — which is why Friday's diplomatic headlines could take 2.4% out of WTI in a session. Expect the $95–$105 band to hold while attacks stay vessel-level; a strike on a Gulf loading facility is the discontinuity. **Natural gas at $2.83/MMBtu is conspicuously absent from the move** — US gas remains a domestic, not a Hormuz, story.

### 2. August CPI and PPI Lock In a September Hike (Impact Score: 31.5)

**Event Date:** Sep 10, 8:30am ET (PPI); Sep 11, 8:30am ET (CPI)
**Event Type:** Economic Data
**News Source:** BLS, CNBC, Kiplinger, NBC News (Official + Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
**CPI rose 0.4% m/m, 3.4% y/y**; **core 0.3% m/m, 2.4% y/y** — a touch above consensus on the core. Gasoline **+3.9% m/m, +27.4% y/y** did the heavy lifting. The day before, **PPI final demand rose 0.4% m/m and 5.4% y/y**, with core (ex food, energy, trade services) **+0.3% m/m, +4.7% y/y**; over three-quarters of the rise was energy. The framing that mattered came from Nationwide's Kathy Bostjancic: *"Chair Warsh and others signaled that interest rates can remain on hold only if disinflation continues and today's August report did not deliver that."*

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate:**
- **Rates:** **10Y 4.96%**, up about **17bp** on the week; **2Y 4.63%**, **30Y 5.36%**. September hike odds moved from **60.6% (Sep 8)** to **85.5% (Sep 12)**, with CME FedWatch quoted near **90%** on Friday.
- **Equities:** **Higher.** S&P 500 **+0.86%**, Nasdaq **+0.96%**, Dow **+0.98%** on Friday — nine of eleven sectors up, tech, industrials and communications each above 1%.
- **Volatility:** **VIX −11.21% to 15.84.**
- **Currencies:** **DXY 99.07**, essentially unchanged (+0.02%) — a striking non-reaction to a 30bp repricing of the front end.

**Follow-Through:** The equity rally is one session against four down days; the rate move is the durable part.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Inverse, and this is the week's central anomaly.** Hot inflation plus a jump to ~90% hike odds classically produces an equity selloff. Instead stocks rallied and the VIX collapsed. Two explanations, both partial: the print *matched* headline expectations after four sessions of pre-positioning for worse, and crude fell 2.4% the same morning, easing the forward inflation path even as the backward-looking print ran hot. Uncertainty resolution beat the level of the data.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Major — a 17bp weekly move in the 10Y sits in the ±10–20bp major band; equity moves were sub-1%.
**Breadth:** Systemic — rates, equities, metals and FX all repriced.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change (+50%) — a hike on Sep 16 is now the base case, not a coin flip. The Fed is tightening into a supply shock.
**Calculated Score:** (7 × 3) × 1.5 = **31.5**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
Rate-sensitives lagged: **utilities and healthcare** were the week's weak spots, with **UnitedHealth −2.5%** and **Amgen −1.3%** among the largest decliners. AI and data-center capex names led on Friday — **Cisco +4.4%, IBM +3.9%, Boeing +2.8%**, with Lumentum, HPE and Ciena alongside.

### 3. Rate Repricing Routs the Metals Complex (Impact Score: 25.0)

**Event Date:** Sep 10–11, 2026
**Event Type:** Commodity / Second-order policy transmission
**News Source:** MINING.COM, USAGOLD, Yahoo Finance (Tier 1 + specialist)

#### Event Summary
The metals took the hit that equities refused to. **Comex December copper fell as much as 5.4% to $6.5160/lb** on Sep 10. **Silver fell 2.58% to $64.90** on Friday, widening the gold/silver ratio to about **67.6**. **Gold spot held $4,385.61** (December futures settled **$4,408.90, +$1.60**) but booked a **third consecutive weekly loss**. The mining-equity rally stopped abruptly.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate:** Copper **Severe**; silver **Major**; gold **Moderate and directionally negative**. Miners sold off with the underlying.

**Follow-Through:** Sustained through Friday's equity rally — metals did not participate in the CPI-day relief.

**Pattern Comparison:** **Consistent with a real-rate shock, inverse to the safe-haven template.** Gold falling for a third week during an escalating shooting war in the Persian Gulf is the anomaly worth sitting with: the real-rate channel is now dominating the geopolitical bid. Silver's industrial leg left it more exposed than gold, exactly as the hierarchy predicts.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — copper's 5.4% move clears the ±4% base-metals severe threshold outright.
**Breadth:** Cross-Asset — base metals, precious metals and mining equities.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation (+25%) — a third straight weekly gold decline is a trend, not a shock.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 2) × 1.25 = **25.0**

**Attribution caveat:** this is largely a *second-order* effect of Event 2 rather than an independent shock, and is ranked separately because the magnitude in copper materially exceeds what the rates move alone explains — a growth signal may be embedded in it.

## Thematic Synthesis

### Dominant Market Narrative
A central bank preparing to tighten into an energy shock it cannot tighten away. Every asset class this week traced back to one loop: Hormuz → crude +9% → PPI energy +4.2% and diesel +24.1% → CPI 3.4% → hike odds ~90% → 30Y at 5.36% → copper −5.4% and gold's third weekly loss.

### Interconnected Events
Events 1 and 2 are **sequential and reinforcing** — the oil move is mechanically inside the inflation prints, which is why a September hike survived a headline CPI that merely matched expectations. Event 3 is a **transmission** of Event 2, not an independent cause. Events 4 and 5 are **offsetting within one sector** and best read as a single dispersion signal.

### Market Regime Assessment
**Risk Appetite:** Mixed, with a hawkish tilt
**Evidence:** Four consecutive down sessions and the Dow's longest losing streak since April argue risk-off; a VIX of 15.84, nine of eleven sectors green Friday and a +0.86% CPI-day rally argue otherwise. The honest read is that equities are absorbing a hawkish repricing while commodities and metals take the strain — the 30Y at 5.36% and copper −5.4% are where the stress actually sits.
**Sector Rotation:** AI/data-center capex (Cisco, IBM, Lumentum, HPE, Ciena) over rate-sensitive defensives (UnitedHealth, Amgen, Merck, utilities). Energy outperformed on crude but lagged badly on Friday as oil reversed.

### Commodity Deep Dive
- **Energy:** WTI **$100.05 (+~9.4% wk)**, Brent **$104.61 (~+9%)**, both reversing 2–3% Friday. Natural gas **$2.83/MMBtu**, flat and decoupled.
- **Precious Metals:** Gold spot **$4,385.61** / Dec futures **$4,408.90**, third straight weekly loss; silver **$64.90 (−2.6% Friday)**. Real rates are overwhelming the war bid.
- **Base Metals:** Copper **−5.4% to $6.5160/lb** — the single largest percentage move in any major asset this week, and the one least explained by the rates story alone.

### Anomalies and Surprises
1. **Stocks rallied on hot inflation.** CPI at 3.4% with hike odds at ~90% produced +0.86% on the S&P and a **−11.2% VIX**. Position-clearing, not data enthusiasm.
2. **Gold fell for a third week during a naval war in the Persian Gulf.** The safe-haven correlation is broken; real rates own the metal right now.
3. **The dollar did nothing.** DXY **99.07, +0.02%** through a 30-point swing in September hike probability — consistent with a market that reads the hike as a *reaction to* a supply shock rather than a sign of US strength.

## Forward-Looking Implications

### Market Positioning Insights
The September hike is now approximately fully priced, so the meeting's risk is entirely in the **dot plot and Chair Warsh's characterization of a supply-driven print** — a hawkish surprise requires more than the hike itself. With copper leading the downside and gold refusing the geopolitical bid, positioning is short duration and short cyclical growth. Equity dispersion between *sold* AI capacity and *monetized* AI software is now the cleanest single-name expression in the market.

### Upcoming Catalysts
- **FOMC decision, dot plot and Warsh press conference · Wed Sep 16**
- **Bank of Japan · Thu Sep 17** — policy rate at 0.75%, the highest since 1995; roughly **62%** priced for a further 25bp, with the 10Y JGB at 2.99%
- **Quadruple witching · Fri Sep 18** — a record-size expiry, the first with CME single-stock futures live, colliding with index rebalancing
- **US retail sales, import/export prices, Empire State · Sep 15–16**

### Risk Scenarios
1. **The Fed hikes and the dots show more.** Probability ~40%. Impact **Major** — the hike is priced; a 2027 dot shift higher is not, and the 30Y at 5.36% has little cushion.
2. **A Gulf energy facility is struck.** Probability ~20%. Impact **Severe** — vessel-level attacks support $100 crude; an export terminal hit is the move to $120 and a genuine equity risk-off.
3. **Copper is signaling growth, not rates.** Probability ~30%. Impact **Major** — a 5.4% break in the macro barometer while the Fed tightens into a supply shock is the classic policy-error setup.
4. **BOJ hikes into quad witching.** Probability ~35%. Impact **Moderate** — a carry unwind landing on a record-size expiry with neutral-to-negative dealer gamma is a volatility accident waiting for a catalyst.
