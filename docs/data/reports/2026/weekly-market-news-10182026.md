## Detailed Event Analysis

### 1. Hormuz Stopped Being a Premium and Started Removing Barrels (Impact Score: 37.5)

**Event Date:** Sunday Oct 11 – Friday Oct 16
**Event Type:** Geopolitical → Commodity supply
**News Source:** UKMTO advisories and Kpler flow data via Tier 1/2 reporting; EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report; IRGC/IRNA state claims (low credibility, flagged)

#### Event Summary
The distinction that defined last week's report — eleven tanker attacks that moved no production — broke this week. **UKMTO counted at least ten vessels attacked in or near the Strait since Sunday Oct 11, described as the highest weekly total since the war began on Feb 28.** More importantly the flow data moved: **Kpler put transits at 20.8 mb on Oct 8 and 2.9 mb on Oct 11**, with the **weekly average at 11.62 mb/d against a 17.15 mb/d baseline — a 32% reduction in the throughput of the chokepoint that carries roughly a fifth of seaborne crude.** Gulf exports are running at **60–80% of normal**, and Iran has publicly tied any reopening to a US withdrawal. The IRGC claimed on Oct 14 that a **container ship sank after striking a naval mine** on what it called an unauthorised route; UKMTO separately reported a **19,000-ton container ship hit by an unidentified projectile** with minor damage. Then the US balance tightened independently: **EIA crude inventories fell 8.6 million barrels in the week to Oct 9, the largest draw since February.**

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (week):**
- **Commodities:** **Brent $109.86 (+4.91% wk)**, **+3.7% on Friday alone**; **WTI $101.41 (+10.4%)** — WTI's first weekly close above $100 in the series of this conflict
- **The spread is the tell:** Brent-WTI compressed from **$12.87 to $8.45**. The premium migrated from waterborne to domestic crude as the 8.6mb draw landed — a US balance tightening, not just a shipping-insurance bid
- **Equities:** **Energy (XLE) +4.86% on the week**, **+2.52% Friday**, the best sector both windows; **XLE is +52.63% YTD**, 2026's best sector
- **Inflation channel:** PPI **energy +10.8%**, accounting for **more than 80%** of the final-goods increase, with **gasoline +20.3%**; CPI **gasoline +9.1% m/m** was the single largest contributor to the headline miss
- **Rates:** oil was cited alongside the AI selloff in Friday's risk-off, but the net weekly move in yields was *lower* (see Rank 4)

**Follow-Through:** **Sustained and accelerating.** Two consecutive weeks of gains with no de-escalation, and the move now has a physical basis rather than a risk premium.

**Pattern Comparison: Consistent, finally.** The reference framework for Hormuz tensions is **oil +5–15% immediately**; WTI's +10.4% sits squarely inside it. For two months escalation bought progressively less because the premium was already embedded. A **32% throughput reduction** is a different variable — the market reprices supply, not fear — which is why this week delivered the textbook magnitude that eleven tanker attacks did not.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — WTI +10.4% is double the 5% severe threshold; Brent +4.91% is at it.
**Breadth:** Systemic — energy, equity sector leadership, and the inflation data that drove Ranks 3 and 4 all trace to it.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — a second consecutive escalation week, now validated by flow and inventory data.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 3.0) × 1.25 = **37.5**

#### Commodity Correlation
**Mechanism:** interdiction raises the cost of transit, then removes the cargo. A 32% throughput cut compounds into the US balance with a lag, which is what the 8.6mb draw represents. **Duration:** Iran's stated condition — a US exit — is not available before the Nov 3 midterms, so the constraint is structural over the window. **Watch:** whether throughput stabilises near 11–12 mb/d or steps down again, and whether the Brent-WTI spread keeps compressing (a US-specific tightening is more inflationary for the CPI than a freight premium).

### 2. The AI Trade Repriced Through Its Credit, Not Its Equity (Impact Score: 30.0)

**Event Date:** Monday Oct 12 – Friday Oct 16
**Event Type:** Corporate / Credit / Sector narrative
**News Source:** Bloomberg, CNN, CNBC, Yahoo Finance; TheStreet (SOX figures)

#### Event Summary
Last week the AI complex lost 3.4% in a session over a revenue *definition*. This week it lost an order of magnitude more over its **cost of capital.** **CoreWeave was the epicentre: its bonds traded at roughly a 15% yield — distressed territory — against the ~6% investment-grade rate its buildout model requires, on approximately $52bn of debt as of Jun 30.** The stock fell **12% Friday and 25% on the week**; CEO Mike Intrator called the bond selloff "irrational," and Nvidia's Jensen Huang said Thursday he remained optimistic, sparking a one-day bounce that did not hold. Two supply-side shocks landed on top: **Bloomberg reported Taiwan will add AI servers and advanced chips to its controlled-items list, making unauthorised shipments to China a criminal offence** under the Foreign Trade Act (no timeline given), and **China's CXMT reported progress on a denser memory design**, reviving oversupply fears across memory names.

### 3. The Hottest Inflation Pair Since 2022 (Impact Score: 26.25)

**Event Date:** Wednesday Oct 14 (CPI), Thursday Oct 15 (PPI, retail sales)
**Event Type:** Economic Data
**News Source:** BLS and Census releases via CNBC, Reuters, CBS, Globe & Mail (Tier 1 reporting of official data)

#### Event Summary
**September CPI on Wednesday printed +0.6% m/m and 3.7% y/y against 3.6% expected, up from 3.4% in August, with core +0.3% m/m versus +0.2% expected and 2.6% y/y. Gasoline, +9.1% m/m, was the main driver.** Thursday's **PPI was the larger surprise: final demand +1.3% m/m against +0.6% consensus — more than double forecast and the largest monthly gain since March 2022 — and +6.3% y/y, the biggest 12-month increase since December 2022**, with August revised up to +0.5%. **Energy rose 10.8%, over 80% of the final-goods increase; gasoline +20.3%; final demand goods +9.8%; core ex food, energy and trade services +5.0% y/y.** The same morning, **September retail sales beat at $745.0bn, +0.8% m/m versus +0.6% expected and +6.3% y/y — but gas stations contributed +6.4%, ex-gas sales rose only +0.2%, and inflation-adjusted spending rose +0.1%.**

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (day-of):**
- **Bonds:** the **10Y spiked from 5.19% to 5.33% within moments** of the CPI release — a ~14bp move, at the top of the Major band
- **Equities:** **S&P 500 −1%**, **Nasdaq −1.9%**, **Technology (XLK) −2.99%** on Wednesday
- **Policy:** **Oct 28 hike odds jumped to 53.7% from 22.1%** on CPI day (CME FedWatch)

**Follow-Through:** **Fully reversed in rates, partially in equities.** By Friday the entire CPI-day yield spike was gone and then some.

**Pattern Comparison: Consistent on the day, Inverse on the week.** The framework for a hot CPI — equities down, yields up sharply, dollar firmer, hawkish repricing — described Wednesday exactly. It then failed completely: a **worse** PPI the next morning produced a **rally** in bonds. The composition explains it. **Over 80% of the PPI increase was energy**, which the Fed's own framework treats as a relative-price shock rather than a demand signal — the "underlying trends" language Williams used on Oct 13, the day before the print. With **real retail sales at +0.1%**, the data describe an economy paying more for fuel and buying nothing else, which is a growth problem the long end prices as such.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Major — a 14bp intraday move on the 10Y and a 1% index day, though both reversed.
**Breadth:** Systemic — rates, equities, FX and the policy path.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — the energy-driven inflation impulse flagged in last week's ISM Services Prices Paid at 74.0 showed up in the official data, on schedule.

### 4. October Went On the Table and Came Straight Back Off (Impact Score: 15.75)

**Event Date:** Tuesday Oct 13 (Williams) – Friday Oct 16 (Warsh), blackout from Saturday Oct 17
**Event Type:** Monetary Policy
**News Source:** Bloomberg (Williams interview transcript), Axios (Warsh), CME FedWatch and Kalshi policy odds

#### Event Summary
Three days of repricing in both directions. **NY Fed President John Williams** told Bloomberg on Oct 13 that he **"[hadn't] changed [his] view that it may be appropriate to have one more rate hike"** but saw **"no reason to believe we would need to do more"** in 2027, expected inflation near 2% that year, and pointed to **"underlying trends"** beneath the energy and AI price shocks. Wednesday's CPI then drove **October hike odds from 22.1% to 53.7%.** By Thursday afternoon they were back to **~28%**, and by Friday to roughly **14–18%** (Kalshi ~14% hike / 87% hold), with Jefferson and Waller having also signalled a possible hold. On Friday, before the blackout opened on Saturday, **Fed Chair Kevin Warsh — sworn in May 22, 2026 — signalled a steady outlook despite the inflation jump.** The **December** path barely moved: **76–89% priced for a hike** (Kalshi 89%, CME ~87.4%) against ~84% a week earlier.

#### Market Reaction

**Immediate (week):**
- **Bonds:** **10Y ~5.09%, −14 to −15bp — the largest weekly decline since the week ended Aug 1, 2025**, with an intraweek range of **5.329% high to 5.037% low**; **30Y 5.43% (~−17bp)**, **2Y 4.72% (~−8bp)**. The curve **bear-flattened in reverse**: the long end fell more than the front, unwinding part of the term-premium story that has driven two months of selloff
- **Currencies:** **DXY ~101.84, roughly −0.3% to −0.4%** — a weaker dollar into hot inflation data, consistent with a growth read rather than a policy read
- **Metals:** **gold $4,181.10 (−0.83% wk)**, and **gold funds saw $1.14bn of outflows, ending a four-week buying streak** — a notable non-confirmation of the risk-off in equities
- **Equities:** Friday's yield relief did not help the Nasdaq and did help the Dow

**Follow-Through:** **Consolidated at the lows in yield.** The front end at 4.72% now prices neither October nor a second hike.

**Pattern Comparison: Inverse, and this is the week's defining anomaly.** The reference pattern for hotter-than-expected CPI and PPI is unambiguous — **yields rise, dollar strengthens, hawkish shift.** All three went the other way on the week. Two mechanisms override the data: the **composition** (over 80% energy, real retail sales +0.1%), and the **bid** — a flight to quality out of Rank 2's credit stress. The Treasury market effectively told the Fed that an energy shock into a zero-real-growth consumer is not a reason to hike six days before a midterm election.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Major — a 15bp weekly move at the largest since August 2025, plus a ~29bp intraweek range.
**Breadth:** Systemic — rates, FX, equities and the policy path.
**Forward Significance:** Contrary Signal — it contradicts both the week's data and the prevailing hawkish narrative.
**Calculated Score:** (7 × 3.0) × 0.75 = **15.75**

## Thematic Synthesis

### Dominant Market Narrative
**The oil price and the AI trade swapped places, and the bond market sided with growth over inflation.** Both of the week's two largest forces pushed inflation higher — Hormuz throughput down 32%, PPI energy +10.8% — yet the 10Y had its best week in over a year. The resolution is that the marginal risk this market prices is no longer the Fed; it is the **cost of capital for the AI buildout**, and that risk expresses itself in credit, which bids Treasuries.

### Interconnected Events
Ranks 1 and 3 are the **same event at two points in the transmission chain**: the Hormuz supply shock is visible as gasoline +20.3% in the PPI and +9.1% in the CPI. Rank 1 is therefore *causally upstream* of the data that briefly put October back in play. Ranks 2 and 4 are also a single mechanism — the flight out of AI credit is the bid in Treasuries — which is why the week's rates move inverted its own data. Rank 5 sits apart but rhymes with Rank 2: in both cases the market ignored strong reported fundamentals and priced the financing conditions instead.

### Market Regime Assessment
**Risk Appetite:** **Risk-off in composition, not in level.**
**Evidence:** The **Dow rose 0.67% to a second straight weekly gain** while the **Nasdaq fell 4.48%** — a ~515bp dispersion that gave the **equal-weighted S&P 500 its best week versus Technology since 2009.** **VIX 19.99 (+5.15 pts)** with a 22.31 intraday high, the highest since Jun 11. Flows confirm the split precisely: **+$13bn into US equity funds** against **−$4.75bn out of technology sector funds.** Not a de-risking — a rotation, executed at size. YTD the index has given back a third of its gain: **S&P ~+11.9% from +14.17%** at the week's start.
**Sector Rotation:** Decisively **out of growth into defensives plus energy**, the same direction as last week but at roughly three times the magnitude. The two-week-old defensive rotation beneath a record index has now broken the index.

### Anomalies and Surprises
1. **The 10Y had its best week since August 2025 in the same week PPI printed its biggest annual gain since December 2022.** The single most important fact in the report, and it says the bond market is pricing the AI credit cycle, not the Fed.
2. **A worse print produced a better reaction.** CPI (a 0.1pt miss) sent the 10Y up 14bp; PPI (a 0.7pt miss, double consensus) sent it to the week's low. Composition beat magnitude.
3. **Gold fell and gold funds bled while the VIX rose 35%.** The safe-haven bid went entirely to Treasuries, which is characteristic of a credit event rather than a macro one.
4. **A 30% earnings beat moved nothing; a bond yield moved $1trn.** Goldman's quarter and CoreWeave's credit spread landed three days apart, and only one of them mattered.
5. **Investors bought the index and sold the sector in the same week** — +$13bn into US equity funds, −$4.75bn out of tech funds. Rotation at this precision is institutional reallocation, not sentiment.

## Forward-Looking Implications

### Market Positioning Insights
The asymmetry has shifted from last week but has not improved. The **good news is now in the bond price** — October effectively priced out at ~14–18%, the 10Y at its best level in two months — while **December still carries 76–89% odds of a hike** and the data that would justify it just printed. The equity market has corrected its *composition* without correcting its *level*: the S&P is 1.95% off a record with the VIX at 19.99, and the sector that caused the drawdown is the one with the unresolved financing question. The live tails are therefore **an AI credit event that spreads past CoreWeave**, and **an October hike against ~85% hold pricing.** Energy is the only leadership with both a physical driver and a flow confirmation.

### Risk Scenarios
1. **AI credit contagion spreads beyond CoreWeave (probability ~30%, impact Severe).** A single borrower's bonds at a 15% yield is idiosyncratic; the same repricing at a hyperscaler's data-centre SPV, a neocloud peer, or in the private-credit funds holding the paper is systemic. The channel is already proven live this week, the VIX is at a four-month high, and the Oct 28–Nov 2 mega-cap capex guidance is the catalyst that tests it.
2. **Hormuz throughput steps down again (probability ~20%, impact Severe).** Flows are already down 32% and Iran has tied reopening to a US exit that cannot happen before Nov 3. A move from 11.6 mb/d toward single digits puts Brent well above $120, forces the energy pass-through the Fed is currently discounting as a relative-price shock, and removes the committee's room to hold.
3. **The Fed hikes on Oct 28 against ~85% hold pricing (probability ~15%, impact Major).** The data support it — 3.7% headline CPI, 6.3% PPI, core PPI +5.0% — and Williams has said one more hike may be appropriate. A hike would undo a 15bp duration rally in hours and arrive with the long end at its most crowded positioning in two months.
4. **December gets priced out instead (probability ~20%, impact Major).** If the AI credit stress deepens and real spending stays near zero, an 87% December hike becomes the most mispriced instrument on the board, with the 2Y at 4.72% the vehicle.
