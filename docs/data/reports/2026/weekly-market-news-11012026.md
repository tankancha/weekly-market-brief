# Weekly Market News — 11/01/2026

## Executive Summary

Analysis period: October 26 – November 1, 2026 (trading week Mon Oct 26 – Fri Oct 30). Thirteen candidate events were screened; five cleared the detailed-analysis threshold. The week's story is not the Fed and not AI demand — it is that **the AI trade broke on cash flow and credit while its revenue lines accelerated**. Microsoft beat with Azure growth accelerating to 48%, AWS grew 39% on a $578bn backlog, and Alphabet's revenue was in line at $127.6bn — yet Alphabet suffered its worst day since its 2004 IPO (−12.6%) on an $8.7bn mark-to-market loss and −$9.1bn quarterly free cash flow, and Amazon fell on a soft Q4 guide and −$42.4bn trailing FCF. Intel then fell 28.6%, its worst session since 1974, on a *PC* guide-down, dragging the SOX 7.2% lower. Critically, the damage was narrow: the S&P 500 lost only 0.57% on the week and the **Russell 2000 rose 1.3%** while the Nasdaq fell 2.0% — money rotated out of AI into Treasuries and small caps rather than out of risk altogether.

## Detailed Event Analysis

### 1. Alphabet Q3 2026 — Growth Without Cash (Impact Score: 25.0)

**Event Date:** Wednesday, October 28, 2026, after hours
**Event Type:** Mega-Cap Earnings
**News Source:** Investing.com transcript, StockTitan, SEC 8-K Ex-99.1 (Tier 1 / Official)

#### Event Summary
Revenue of **$127.6bn (~+25% y/y) was in line** — this was not a demand miss. GAAP EPS came in at **$2.12 against ~$3.01 consensus, a 29.5% shortfall**, driven by an **$8.7bn loss on equity securities**, attributed by the CFO principally to the SpaceX stake. Q3 capex was **$51.4bn** and FY26 capex guidance was left **unchanged at $195–205bn**. The decisive line was **quarterly free cash flow of −$9.1bn**.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate:** −10% after hours Oct 28; **closed Oct 29 down 12.6% at $283.82**, its largest one-day decline since the 2004 listing, pushing the stock back into the red for 2026 after having been +4% YTD against the S&P's +13%.
- **Equities:** This single name drove the Nasdaq's −1.38% on Oct 29 **even as breadth was positive** — health care, real estate and staples led, and a majority of S&P 500 constituents rose.
- **Rates:** 10Y bid on safe-haven demand, ending the week at 4.93% (−7bp); 2Y −10bp to 4.59%.
- **Volatility:** VIX began the climb toward Friday's 19.42.

**Follow-Through:** Sustained; Friday compounded rather than reversed the move.

**Pattern Comparison: Amplified.** Capex came in *below* the ~$55bn consensus and revenue was in line. Historically that combination is rewarded. The market instead punished earnings quality and cash conversion — confirming the inversion that began with Meta on October 22.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — 12.6% in a top-five index weight.
**Breadth:** Cross-Asset — equities, hyperscaler credit sentiment, safe-haven rates bid.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation (+25%) — validates rather than creates the Meta signal.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 2.0) × 1.25 = **25.0**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
Communication Services took the direct hit, with read-through to AI infrastructure suppliers and the power/datacenter complex, whose equity stories are underwritten by hyperscaler capex that is now visibly cash-negative.

### 2. Intel Q4 Guidance — AI Eats the PC Market (Impact Score: 22.5)

**Event Date:** Thursday, October 29, 2026 (a/h) → Friday, October 30 reaction
**Event Type:** Corporate Earnings / Guidance
**News Source:** CNBC, TheStreet (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
Q3 was a **beat** — adjusted EPS **$0.52 vs $0.41** expected. The guidance was the problem: **Q4 revenue of $15–16bn against ~$17bn consensus and Q4 adjusted EPS of $0.25 against $0.43**. The cause is AI *crowding-out*, not AI weakness: memory prices bid up by datacenter buildouts have made PCs materially costlier to build, with BofA's Vivek Arya citing **PC shipments down 25–30% quarter-on-quarter in Q4**. Intel's own AI server business grew **78%** and is supply-constrained.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate (Day-of):** INTC fell from $92.07 to **$65.76, −28.6%**, its worst day since 1974 (reported figures range from −21% to −29.6% depending on intraday vs close-to-close; the close-to-close figure is ~−28.6%). Contagion was broad: **Dell −13%, AMD −10%, ARM −10.2%, Nvidia −3%, SOX −7.2%**. Index-level: S&P 500 −1.82% to **7,580.07** (worst day since June 5), Nasdaq −2.9% to **25,437.00**, Dow −1.1% to **51,770.31**. VIX +12.91% to 19.42 (intraday high 23.93).

**Follow-Through:** BofA cut to Neutral from Buy; several analysts called the move overdone. **This, not the Fed, was the proximate cause of Friday's selloff.**

**Pattern Comparison: Amplified.** A guidance miss of this shape normally costs 10–15%. The 28.6% outcome reflects a market actively hunting second-order evidence that AI capex destroys value elsewhere in the tech stack — and finding it in a company that beat on the quarter.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — 28.6% in a single session.
**Breadth:** Sector-Wide — semis, PC OEMs, memory, hardware.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change (+50%) — introduces a new mechanism: AI input-cost inflation transmitting into non-AI tech margins.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 1.5) × 1.50 = **22.5**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
Memory makers are the structural winners of the same dynamic that destroys PC-levered margins. Expect dispersion *within* semis to widen rather than the sector to trade as one beta.

### 3. FOMC Holds at 3.75–4.00% With Two Hike Dissents (Impact Score: 18.0)

**Event Date:** Wednesday, October 28, 2026, 14:00 ET
**Event Type:** Monetary Policy
**News Source:** federalreserve.gov, CNBC, CNN, Yahoo Finance live (Official / Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
The Committee voted **10–2** to hold the target range at 3.75–4.00%. **Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favor of a 25bp hike** — the dissent configuration, not the hold, is the signal. The statement called inflation "elevated" and said the Committee awaits "accumulation of more information." **Chair Kevin Warsh** said the midterms played "no role," judged that September's hike (the first since July 2023) is curbing aggregate demand, and flagged 2027 calendar changes that could reduce the number of rate-setting meetings. October is a non-projection meeting; the next SEP comes in December.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate (Day-of):**
- **Equities:** Muted — S&P 500 closed **7,743.98, +0.28%**; Dow +0.2%; Nasdaq-100 roughly flat minutes after the release
- **Bonds:** Bear-steepened intraday; by week's end **2Y 4.59% (−10bp), 10Y 4.93% (−7bp), 30Y 5.32%**, with 2s10s +3bp to 0.33%
- **Currencies:** Dollar firmed on the statement then slipped ~0.2% during the presser; **DXY ended at 101.96, flat on the week**
- **Commodities:** No material direct impact

**Follow-Through:** The durable output was the rate path — a December hike was priced near **84% immediately after the decision, easing to roughly 70–80% by week's end**. Next decision December 8–9, with new projections.

**Pattern Comparison: Dampened** on the day, by design. Warsh's refusal to pre-commit let both camps read the presser their way, suppressing the immediate move while leaving a hawkish skeleton.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Moderate — modest 10Y move but a meaningful front-end and curve repricing.
**Breadth:** Systemic — rates, FX, equity discount rates, commodity carry.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change (+50%) — the Fed is in a live hiking cycle.
**Calculated Score:** (4 × 3.0) × 1.50 = **18.0**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
A hawkish bias is a headwind for long-duration growth equities precisely as their cash-flow story deteriorates — the week's two dominant themes reinforce each other.

### 5. Hormuz Mine Strikes and Houthi Attacks on Saudi Refineries (Impact Score: 15.0)

**Event Date:** October 26–31, 2026
**Event Type:** Geopolitical / Commodity
**News Source:** CNBC, AP/US News, UKMTO, Gulf News (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
Iran's IRGC claimed **four oil tankers struck naval mines** in the Strait of Hormuz on "unauthorized routes"; **US CENTCOM disputed this**, saying it had cleared mines from primary transit routes, and AP could not independently confirm Iran's account. Separately **UKMTO reported a tanker struck by explosive devices attached by a small boat** — reportedly the first successful limpet-mine strike in the waterway since the war began in February, attribution unknown. The **Houthis claimed strikes on the Ras Tanura and Rabigh refineries and the Abqaiq facility**, plus six operations across Saudi Arabia, despite a rare senior Saudi–Iranian meeting in Tehran that week; Saudi Arabia reported interceptions including a drone near Mecca. Iran's Supreme Leader ordered an unrestrained response to any attack, while the foreign ministry called a US dialogue effort "a positive step." The US had ordered non-emergency government staff out of Saudi Arabia effective October 23.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate:** Oil rose more than 2% Friday to its highest settle since late April — expiring December Brent **$117.64**, January front-month **$113.43**, WTI **$104.12**.

**Follow-Through:** The monthly figure is the story — **Brent +13.6% and WTI +15.2% in October**, a fourth consecutive monthly gain. OPEC+ core members met Sunday November 1 and were reported to have agreed in principle to hold December targets; most Gulf members already pump below quota because of war-related export disruption, so a freeze changes little physically. Attention turns to 2027 baselines.

**Pattern Comparison: Dampened.** Confirmed mine strikes on tankers plus claimed hits on three Saudi facilities would ordinarily be worth 5%+ on Brent. The expiring contract rose under 1% on the week. The market has repriced Hormuz from an event into a *standing condition*.

#### Commodity Correlation
The key tell: **Saudi crude exports actually rose in October versus September** per ship-tracking firms. This is a risk premium, not realized supply loss — which is precisely why the weekly move was contained even as the monthly move was large. The transmission now runs oil→PCE→policy rates→equity discount rates rather than oil→equities: September headline PCE at 3.6% is substantially an energy artifact, and it is what put two hike dissents on the FOMC and a discussed hike in the ECB's room. Duration risk is high because the binding constraint is a naval blockade, not a production decision, so neither OPEC+ nor US shale can resolve it on a tradable horizon.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Moderate — large monthly move, contained weekly move.
**Breadth:** Systemic — energy, inflation prints, every central bank reaction function.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation (+25%) — escalation of an established regime.
**Calculated Score:** (4 × 3.0) × 1.25 = **15.0**

## Thematic Synthesis

### Dominant Market Narrative
**AI demand accelerated; AI cash flow deteriorated; the market priced the second.** Microsoft's FQ1 was the cleanest counter-evidence to a demand thesis — revenue $92.28bn (+19%), adjusted EPS $4.89 vs $4.73, **Azure +48%** (disclosed in dollars for the first time at $33.17bn) with Q2 guidance of 49–50% constant-currency against 45% consensus, and free cash flow of $18.6bn, roughly $10bn above consensus, though flattered by GPU delivery timing. Capex still roughly doubled year-on-year to ~$39.4bn. Set against that, Alphabet's −$9.1bn quarterly FCF, Amazon's −$42.4bn trailing FCF and Meta's negative FCF the prior week establish the pattern: the buildout now consumes more cash than the businesses generate, and the equity market has switched its objective function from spend discipline to cash conversion.

### The Credit Channel — This Week's Most Important Underreported Signal
The funding side corroborated the equity move. **Senior AAA data-center CMBS spreads widened 21–24bp in the week ended October 28 (Trepp)** — widening that reached investment-grade tranches which would normally absorb such stress. Supporting evidence: at least **18 high-yield data-center deals priced at double-digit yields**, with **Volta Infrastructure's $5bn syndicated loan clearing above 11%**; **CoreWeave 5Y CDS hit a record ~1,140–1,195bp** and **Oracle 5Y CDS a record ~282–288bp**; and AI-related issuance is now **~4% of the Bloomberg US HY index but ~40% of 2026 net new issuance**, with AI-issuer spreads near **115bp against ~78bp for broad IG**. Weekly recaps explicitly attributed the Treasury rally to the AI equity selloff *and* surging credit risk premiums. The counterpoint on record: CNBC reported on October 27 that AI debt fears may have been overblown after Meta's equity offering calmed nerves.

### Interconnected Events
Reinforcing, which is why a −0.57% weekly tape understates the dispersion. The energy shock drives inflation; inflation keeps the Fed hawkish; a hawkish Fed raises the discount rate on exactly the long-duration cash flows that Alphabet and Amazon just pushed further out. Intel is the second-order link — AI datacenter demand bid up memory, which gutted PC economics — proving the capex cycle has victims inside its own sector. The energy shock ranks only fifth precisely because it is now fully absorbed as a standing condition; the week's *marginal* information was all in corporate cash flow and credit spreads.

### Market Regime Assessment
**Risk Appetite:** Mixed — a narrow, funding-driven de-rating, not broad risk aversion.
**Evidence:** The divergence is the whole point. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.0% on the week and 5.3% in October (worst month since March 2025; Nasdaq 100 −6.65%), and the SOX fell 7.2% on Friday alone — but the **Russell 2000 rose 1.3%**, breadth on October 29 was positive even as the index fell, and the S&P 500 lost just 0.57% (its third consecutive weekly decline, the longest run since March, leaving it ~3% below its October 6 record and still +10.7% YTD). The 10Y *rallied* 7bp to 4.93% and the 2Y 10bp to 4.59%. Capital rotated from AI into Treasuries and small caps rather than fleeing risk.
**Sector Rotation:** Defensives and cyclicals over mega-cap growth — health care, real estate and staples led Thursday; the Dow gained roughly 1.4–1.7% in October while the Nasdaq lost over 5%. Energy was the month's beneficiary. Within semis, memory separated from PC-levered names.

### Commodity Deep Dive
- **Energy:** Brent Dec settled $117.64 (+0.8% wk, +13.6% Oct); Jan front-month $113.43; WTI $104.12 (+15.2% Oct). Fourth consecutive monthly gain, highest since late April. Driver is supply-route risk premium, not realized supply loss.
- **Precious Metals:** Gold **broke below $3,900 to ~$3,876–3,897, a one-year low**, roughly −3% on the week and −6% to −7% in October — its **worst October since 2008** — and about 30% below the January record near $5,595. Silver broke $50 to ~$48.90–49.09. Gold fell *alongside* equities, the signature of a rate shock rather than risk aversion.
- **Base Metals:** Copper ~$6.45–6.51/lb, −2% Friday. Not the marginal driver.
- **Agricultural:** Not a factor this week.

### Anomalies and Surprises
1. **Gold sold off into an equity selloff.** Simultaneous declines in gold and equities with a Treasury bid points to December hike pricing and forced deleveraging, not fear.
2. **Alphabet fell 12.6% on in-line revenue and a capex *beat*.** Spending came in below consensus and the stock had its worst day in 22 years. The market's objective function has shifted decisively to cash conversion.
3. **Intel beat on the quarter and fell 28.6%.** The entire move was guidance, and the guidance problem was an input-cost externality created by its own industry's AI buildout.
4. **The Russell 2000 rose while the Nasdaq fell 2%.** A genuine rotation, not a risk-off — and the single strongest argument that this de-rating is still orderly.
5. **A formal Hormuz closure and claimed refinery strikes moved Brent under 1% on the week** while Saudi exports rose. Near-complete desensitization to headlines that would have been worth 5%+ a year ago.

## Forward-Looking Implications

### Market Positioning Insights
The index-level damage remains shallow relative to the internals — the S&P is down 0.57% on the week against a 6.65% monthly decline in the Nasdaq 100. That gap is the risk: the S&P has not yet priced what its largest constituents' cash flows are signaling. Note the inverted inflation gap — **core CPI at 2.6% against core PCE at 3.1%** — a historically unusual configuration that complicates the December call. The AI complex must now clear two tests at once: a December hike priced near 70–80%, and a Q4 reporting cycle in which 2027 capex guidance lands against visibly negative free cash flow. Reported trimming of OpenAI IPO valuation targets by $150bn is private-market confirmation of the same repricing.

### Upcoming Catalysts
- **ISM Manufacturing (Oct) and Apple FQ4 results — Monday, November 2** (Apple guided +9–11% y/y; consensus runs higher near $113bn)
- **US midterm elections — Tuesday, November 3** (Kalshi ~93% Democratic House; options imply only ~1.2x a normal day's volatility, with a contested count the real tail risk)
- **AMD and Qualcomm earnings, week of Nov 2–6** — direct read-across to the Intel-triggered semis de-rating
- **October jobs report — Friday, November 6** (September was +29k, unemployment 4.2%)
- **October CPI — Tuesday, November 10** (core CPI last 2.6%; Cleveland Fed nowcasts October core PCE ~+0.26% m/m)
- **Nvidia FQ3 earnings — Tuesday, November 17** (consensus EPS $2.49; the single biggest scheduled test of the AI-capex-to-credit thread)
- **FOMC decision with new projections — December 8–9** (~70–80% hike priced); **ECB December 17** (~67% hike priced)

### Risk Scenarios
1. **AI capex credit event (30%, Severe):** Three of the four top hyperscalers are now FCF-negative while guiding 2027 capex higher, AAA datacenter CMBS is widening, and AI paper is 40% of HY net issuance against 4% of the index. A failed datacenter financing converts an equity de-rating into a credit event.
2. **December hike into decelerating growth (35%, Major):** With a hike ~70–80% priced on 3.1% core PCE, a soft November 6 payroll forces a choice between the inflation mandate and a growth scare — either resolution repriced.
3. **Hormuz escalation beyond bypass capacity (20%, Severe):** Bypass routes and rising Saudi exports have absorbed the closure so far. A strike on a bypass pipeline or a confirmed tanker loss would break the desensitization and send Brent through $130.
4. **Rotation fails and the de-rating broadens (25%, Major):** The Russell's outperformance is the load-bearing evidence that this is orderly. If small caps roll over while AI keeps de-rating, the S&P's 0.57% weekly loss reprices toward the Nasdaq's.
