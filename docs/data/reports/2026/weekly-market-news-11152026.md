# Weekly Market News — 11/15/2026

## Executive Summary

Analysis period 11/09–11/15/2026 (trading week Nov 9–13); five Tier-1 events identified. The S&P 500 rose 2.5% to 7,561.67 — its best week since early August — snapping a four-week losing streak, with all eleven sectors higher and the VIX down roughly 25% to 19.30. The rebound happened despite a genuine rate shock: October CPI beat on Nov 10 (core +0.3% vs +0.2% expected), the 10-year Treasury yield climbed about 30bp to ~5.14%, breaking back above 5% for the first time since late October, and the dollar hit a 19-month high. The two highest-impact events were that CPI print and Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO on Nov 12 (NVDA −12.75%, a sixth straight weekly decline). The week's defining feature was internal: leadership at the top of the index changed hands, with Apple overtaking Nvidia as the largest US company, while semiconductors as a group still gained 8%.

## Detailed Event Analysis

### 1. October CPI Beats, December Hike All But Locked (Impact Score: 26.25)

**Event Date:** Tuesday, Nov 10, 2026, 08:30 ET
**Event Type:** Economic Data / Monetary Policy
**News Source:** BLS (official); CNBC, Reuters (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
Headline CPI rose 0.4% m/m and core CPI 0.3% versus 0.2% consensus, with shelter contributing over 30% of the increase. The BLS published **no 12-month rate** — the October 2025 government shutdown destroyed the comparison base, leaving September's 3.7% as the last official annual print; street estimates of the true YoY rate cluster at 3.8–4.0%. October PPI followed on Nov 13: +0.3% m/m, *below* the 0.4% consensus and far slower than September's 1.4%, but the annual rate rose to 6.5% from 6.3%, the fastest since late 2022, with wholesale food up 1.3% — the most since July 2022. Pipeline pressure is now food-led, not energy-led, which is the harder problem for the Fed.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate (Day-of):** Equities — S&P 500 −0.38% to 7,520.93, Dow −0.30%; chips led the decline (Micron, Marvell each −3%). Rates — the 10-year rose on the day and ~30bp on the week to ~5.14%. FX — dollar bid. Breadth near even, with the equal-weight S&P roughly flat.
**Follow-Through:** Fully absorbed within 24 hours. Wednesday closed at 7,619.38, the week's high.
**Pattern Comparison:** **Dampened in equities, consistent elsewhere.** The reference pattern (higher CPI → equities down, yields up, dollar up, gold mixed) held directionally in every asset, but the equity magnitude was trivial relative to a repricing that added a December hike and opened a 50bp tail. Positioning explains it: equities had already fallen four straight weeks, and Monday's +2.35% rally had banked the week's entire gain before the print landed.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Major — small equity moves, but a ~30bp weekly move in the 10-year and a wholesale repricing of the global rate path.
**Breadth:** Systemic — equities, bonds, FX and precious metals all repriced; Asia FX followed.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — ratifies the hiking inversion begun by the prior week's +165K payrolls.
**Calculated Score:** (7 × 3.0) × 1.25 = **26.25**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
Fed pricing moved to ~88.8% for 25bp on Dec 9 (from ~72%), with 50bp odds rising from zero to 13.6%, later 14.5%. A Reuters poll had all 101 economists expecting 25bp. Officials turned uniformly hawkish — Logan backs two more hikes, Williams called inflation "far too high," Hammack urged a restrictive stance, Schmid said inflation is undefeated; only Goolsbee dissented in tone, calling the report "distressing." Waller had pre-committed to considering 50bp if core printed ≥0.3% — it did.

### 2. Jensen Huang Resigns as Nvidia CEO (Impact Score: 22.50)

**Event Date:** 8-K accepted Nov 11; announced Thursday, Nov 12, 2026
**Event Type:** Corporate / Governance
**News Source:** NVIDIA newsroom + SEC Form 8-K (official); CNBC, Axios, PBS (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
Nvidia's board announced Huang had resigned as President, CEO and director, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO while retaining the CFO role; Harvey C. Jones was named the company's first board chair. No reason was given. Huang had admitted to extramarital affairs, and reporting tied the departure to a board investigation involving a woman allegedly connected to smuggling advanced AI chips into China — **reported, not confirmed**; China denies the account. The Senate signalled the resignation does not end its national-security inquiry.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate:** NVDA fell to $148.80 after Thursday's open, −14.4% from the prior close, then recovered to close −4.34% at $166.19; it lost a further 2.01% Friday to $162.85. On the week, −12.75% — a **sixth consecutive weekly decline, the longest streak since 2012** — closing below a $4tn market cap for the first time since July 2025. Friday saw the sector follow-through: SOX −3.4% (worst day since Nov 5), AMD −9%, Technology the only sector down more than 1%.
**Follow-Through:** Sustained at the stock level, reversed at the sector level — a divergence, not a recovery.
**Pattern Comparison:** **Inverse at the sector level.** The reference pattern for a mega-cap shock is sector-wide contagion dragging the index. Instead the SOX *gained 8.05%* on the week (SOXX +9.35%), led by AMD +19% and Broadcom +18% — on Thursday itself, as Nvidia fell, AMD rose 6.14% and Broadcom 5.24%. Investors treated a governance failure as a transfer of franchise value to competitors, not as an indictment of AI demand.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — a −14.4% intraday move in what began the week as the index's largest weight.
**Breadth:** Sector-Wide — semis and the AI complex repriced; rates and FX did not respond.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change — a leadership vacuum at the most systemically important stock in the market, under an unresolved national-security investigation, three trading days before earnings.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 1.5) × 1.50 = **22.50**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
At least nine firms cut Nvidia ratings or targets in 48 hours: Bernstein to Market Perform with a target halved to $200 (from $400), Evercore ISI to In Line at $186 (from $465), Baird to Hold at $200, HSBC to Hold at $170 citing succession and export risk; Oppenheimer and William Blair downgraded, and Morgan Stanley removed NVDA as its top semiconductor pick under a stop-loss policy. Meanwhile **Apple closed at a record $355.63 on Nov 11 (+2.04%), a ~$5.17tn market cap, overtaking Nvidia (~$4.19tn) as the largest US company** — the single clearest explanation of how the S&P held up while chips sold off. Nvidia reports Q3 FY2027 on Nov 17 after the close against prior guidance of $108bn ±2% (consensus ~$109bn) and 74% gross margin, with a 71–72% margin trough already flagged for the January quarter on rising memory costs — now to be defended by an interim CEO.

### 3. Dollar Hits 19-Month High; Yen Breaches 160 (Impact Score: 15.00)

**Event Date:** Thursday–Friday, Nov 12–13, 2026
**Event Type:** FX / Monetary Policy
**News Source:** Reuters (Tier 1); Japan Ministry of Finance statements (official)

#### Event Summary
The dollar index closed at 103.41, its highest since April 2025, driven by the widening Fed–rest-of-world policy gap. USD/JPY briefly breached 160 on Thursday before settling at 159.875. Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama said the government would "respond appropriately" to volatility — the standard verbal precursor to intervention, and a line Japan has already backed with action twice in 2026. The BoJ remains at 1.25% after holding on Oct 30, leaving the yen a funding currency.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate:** DXY +0.23% Friday to a 19-month high; Asia FX held losses; gold and silver were liquidated.
**Follow-Through:** Sustained, with two-way risk — the rate gap pulls the dollar up while intervention threat can erase a week's move in one session.
**Pattern Comparison:** **Consistent and amplified** — this was the channel through which the CPI shock reached commodities.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Moderate — a level and threshold story, not a magnitude story; daily moves were small.
**Breadth:** Systemic — DXY, JPY, Asia FX, precious metals, and the Japanese policy reaction function.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation.
**Calculated Score:** (4 × 3.0) × 1.25 = **15.00**

### 4. AI-Infrastructure Earnings Split: Delivery Rewarded, Capex Sold (Impact Score: 13.13)

**Event Date:** Tuesday–Friday, Nov 10–13, 2026
**Event Type:** Corporate Earnings
**News Source:** CNBC, Benzinga, Investing.com (Tier 1 / specialized)

#### Event Summary
Three AI-infrastructure prints landed and the market sorted them ruthlessly. **CoreWeave** (Nov 10) reported revenue of $3.661bn, +168% y/y, beating $3.55bn, with adjusted operating income of $379m against $200–260m guidance and backlog up to $132.5bn from $104bn at end-June; it raised FY26 revenue guidance to $13.0–13.3bn and guided to a $20–21bn exit run-rate. **Cisco** (Nov 12) beat on revenue of $18.52bn (+24% y/y) and EPS of $1.40, and raised FY27 adjusted EPS guidance roughly 8% to $5.47–5.53 while lifting hyperscaler AI-infrastructure revenue to $9bn. **Applied Materials** (Nov 12) posted record revenue of $10.41bn, +53% y/y, and guided FQ1 above consensus.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate:** CoreWeave rose about 24% in the following session and was the proximate catalyst for Wednesday's broad AI rally (S&P +1.31%, Nasdaq +1.60%). Cisco rose 1.9% after hours, then fell 6.2% to $95.64 on Friday, round-tripping to roughly its pre-print level despite the beat-and-raise. Applied Materials traded as high as $389 after hours before closing down 3.4% at $367.76, having already fallen ~20% over the prior month.
**Pattern Comparison:** **Inverse for the capex names.** The reference pattern is "beat + raise → strong positive." Two beat-and-raises were sold instead. The market is paying for demonstrated AI *revenue delivery* and selling AI *capex exposure* — commentary attributed the Cisco selling to doubts about demand durability and order deceleration, not to the quarter itself.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Major — drove the week's second-best session and produced a 24% single-stock move.
**Breadth:** Sector-Wide — technology and semis; no rates or FX response.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — reinforces the post-election de-rating of AI capex while validating AI revenue.
**Calculated Score:** (7 × 1.5) × 1.25 = **13.13**

### 5. IEA Flags First Post-War Oil Surplus Against Houthi Strikes (Impact Score: 12.00)

**Event Date:** Monday–Friday, Nov 9–13, 2026
**Event Type:** Commodity / Geopolitical
**News Source:** IEA (official); Reuters, CNBC (Tier 1); Al Jazeera

#### Event Summary
The IEA's Nov 13 report put Q4 supply at 105mb/d — 1.7mb/d above demand and the first surplus since the Iran war began — with October oversupply near 2mb/d and a 3.8mb/d surplus projected for 2027. Against this, the Houthis claimed strikes on the Yanbu facility, an East-West Pipeline pumping station and an Aramco building in Riyadh on Nov 12, following a Nov 9 pipeline attack that sparked a fire without halting operations. The pipeline matters disproportionately: it is Saudi Arabia's main route bypassing Iran's Strait of Hormuz blockade. Separately, Trump said on Nov 12 that the US "could do without" an Iran deal and would keep the blockade; Iran refused to surrender "a single atom" of enriched material.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate:** Brent settled $95.84 (−2.52%, −$2.48) and WTI $88.56 (−2.50%, −$2.27) — a second consecutive weekly decline after the prior week's 16%/13% collapse. Thursday's attacks lifted Brent $3.06 and WTI $2.21 intraday.
**Pattern Comparison:** **Severely dampened.** The 2019 Aramco attacks moved oil +15% in a single day; comparable infrastructure strikes this week produced roughly +3%, fully surrendered by Friday. A 1.7mb/d surplus is absorbing geopolitical risk premium that would have been explosive twelve months ago.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Moderate — −2.5% weekly with a +3% intraweek geopolitical spike.
**Breadth:** Cross-Asset — energy, energy equities, inflation expectations.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change — "first surplus since the war began" marks the end of scarcity pricing.
**Calculated Score:** (4 × 2.0) × 1.50 = **12.00**

#### Commodity Correlation
Oil fell on OPEC+ raising October output, rising US crude and fuel stocks, and the blockade's paradoxical effect of keeping Iranian barrels off-market while the rest of the world oversupplies. Note the dislocation: the Energy *sector* led all S&P sectors at +5.4% while crude fell 2.5% — equity investors bid Saudi supply risk and the prior week's oversold condition, not the spot price.

## Thematic Synthesis

### Dominant Market Narrative
Equities absorbed a real rate shock. The S&P gained 2.5% in the same week the 10-year broke back above 5% and a December hike became near-certain — the market has stopped treating a tightening Fed as existential and started discriminating violently *within* sectors. Monday alone (+2.35%, the best session since April 8) delivered the entire weekly gain; the four sessions that followed the CPI print were a controlled, chip-led bleed.

### Interconnected Events
Events 1 and 3 are one chain: the CPI beat repriced the Fed path, lifting the 10-year ~30bp and the dollar to a 19-month high, which crushed gold and silver. Events 2 and 4 ran orthogonally and compounded each other — Nvidia's governance shock and the selling of Cisco's and Applied Materials' beat-and-raises are the same trade, a market rotating out of AI capex exposure and into AI revenue delivery and competitive share. Event 5 cut against Event 1's inflation impulse: a 1.7mb/d oil surplus is actively disinflationary and smothered a supply shock that historically moves crude 15%.

### Market Regime Assessment
**Risk Appetite:** Risk-On — rate-shock tolerant, but internally fractured
**Evidence:** VIX down ~25% to 19.30 (Wednesday low close 17.49); S&P +2.5% snapping a four-week losing streak with **all eleven sectors higher**, Energy (+5.4%) and Industrials (+3.7%) leading and Materials the laggard (~+0.5%); Russell 2000 +1.3% to 2,790.49; positive Friday breadth (~314 advancers vs ~185 decliners) despite the index falling. Gold and silver fell on real-rate repricing, not fear.
**Sector Rotation:** Cyclicals over defensives at the index level. Within technology, a violent rotation from Nvidia (−12.75%) to AMD (+19%) and Broadcom (+18%), leaving SOX +8.05% — and a handover at the very top, with Apple displacing Nvidia as the largest US company.

### Commodity Deep Dive
- **Energy:** WTI $88.56 (−2.50%), Brent $95.84 (−2.52%); second weekly decline on the IEA surplus, higher OPEC+ output and rising US stocks. XLE +45% YTD remains the year's leading sector.
- **Precious Metals:** Gold lost $4,000, closing $3,884.69 after a $185.90 (−4.57%) weekly drop — its worst since early June. Silver fell 6.10% to $49.93 spot, with COMEX futures settling $48.65, a one-year low. Dollar strength and hike repricing overrode both the inflation hedge and an active Middle East supply threat.
- **Base Metals:** Materials was the weakest S&P sector, consistent with dollar strength, though it still finished higher.

### Anomalies and Surprises
1. **Semis rose 8% while Nvidia fell 13%.** A governance collapse at the AI trade's centre produced rotation, not contagion — the most important signal of the week.
2. **Stocks had their best week since August while the 10-year rose ~30bp above 5%.** An unusual pairing; equities are no longer trading as a pure duration asset.
3. **Two AI beat-and-raises were sold.** Cisco (−6.2%) and Applied Materials (−3.4%) both beat and raised guidance and both fell.
4. **Gold fell 4.6% during live attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure.** The geopolitical-premium framework inverted; real rates dominated.
5. **Energy equities led (+5.4%) while crude fell 2.5%**, and infrastructure strikes moved oil ~3% versus +15% for the 2019 Aramco precedent.

### Corporate Backdrop
Deal flow stayed heavy: NRG agreed to sell six gas plants (~4.9GW) to LS Power for $4.0bn cash, funding $3bn of buybacks and $2bn of debt reduction; Hancock Whitney agreed to acquire Trustmark in a ~$3.2bn all-stock merger creating a ~$58bn-asset Southeastern bank; and SpaceX committed more than $74bn to two AI supercomputing campuses in Texas and Arizona, subject to local approvals with hearings set for Dec 9.

## Forward-Looking Implications

### Market Positioning Insights
The four-week downtrend broke, but leadership has narrowed and changed hands. With the S&P up 10.5% YTD, a December hike fully priced and the 10-year above 5%, the asymmetry now sits in the 50bp tail — unpriced a week ago, ~14.5% now. Precious metals are the cleanest expression of the hawkish path; a hot PCE would extend the liquidation. Nvidia's six-week losing streak against a record-high Apple is the cleanest expression of the AI rotation.

### Upcoming Catalysts
- **Fed Chair Warsh speech** — Mon Nov 16, 12:00 ET (San Francisco Fed conference)
- **Nvidia Q3 FY2027 earnings** — Tue Nov 17 after the close, first report under interim CEO Kress
- **Home Depot (Nov 17), Walmart (Nov 19), Disney (Nov 17)** — consumer read-through

### Risk Scenarios
1. **Nvidia miss or guidance cut under interim leadership (~30%, Severe):** Margin trough already flagged at 71–72%; no permanent CEO to defend the capex narrative, and nine sell-side targets were cut into the print.
2. **Japanese FX intervention above USD/JPY 160 (~35%, Major):** Explicit MoF warnings with two 2026 precedents; would reverse the dollar trade violently in one session.
3. **50bp December hike (~15%, Severe):** Waller's stated precondition has been met. Would hit long-duration equities and gold hardest.
4. **10-year sustained above 5.25% (~25%, Major):** Equities absorbed 5.14% this week; a further leg would test that tolerance.
5. **Iran escalation closing the East-West Pipeline (~15%, Major):** The surplus is absorbing strikes now, but the bypass route around Hormuz is the one genuine chokepoint left.
