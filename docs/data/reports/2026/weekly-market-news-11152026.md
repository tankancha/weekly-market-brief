# Weekly Market News — 11/15/2026

## Executive Summary

Analysis period 11/09–11/15/2026 (trading week Nov 9–13); five Tier-1 events identified. The S&P 500 rose 2.51% to 7,561.67 — its best week since early August — snapping a four-week losing streak, with the VIX collapsing 24.73% to 19.30. The rebound happened *through* a hawkish shock, not in its absence: October CPI beat on Nov 10 (core +0.3% vs +0.2% expected), pushing December hike odds to ~89% and lifting the dollar to a 19-month high. The two highest-impact events were that CPI print and Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO on Nov 12 (NVDA −12.75% on the week). Critically, capital rotated *within* the AI trade rather than out of it — the SOX semiconductor index gained 8.05% even as its largest member lost nearly 13%.

## Detailed Event Analysis

### 1. October CPI Beats, December Hike All But Locked (Impact Score: 26.25)

**Event Date:** Tuesday, Nov 10, 2026, 08:30 ET
**Event Type:** Economic Data / Monetary Policy
**News Source:** BLS (official); CNBC, Reuters (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
Headline CPI rose 0.4% m/m and core CPI 0.3% versus 0.2% consensus, with shelter contributing over 30% of the increase. The BLS published **no 12-month rate** — the October 2025 government shutdown destroyed the comparison base, leaving September's 3.7% as the last official annual print. Street estimates of the true YoY rate cluster at 3.8–4.0%. The miss was small in absolute terms but arrived with the Fed already mid-pivot toward tightening.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate (Day-of):** Equities — S&P 500 −0.6%, Nasdaq −1.0%; chips led the decline (Micron, Marvell each −3%). Rates — Treasury yields jumped. FX — dollar bid. Volatility — contained; VIX still closed the week far lower.
**Follow-Through:** Consolidated, then reversed. Wednesday's close of 7,619.38 was the week's high, so the CPI dip was fully absorbed within 24 hours.
**Pattern Comparison:** **Dampened.** The reference pattern (higher-than-expected CPI → equities negative, yields up, dollar up) held directionally in every asset, but the equity magnitude was trivial relative to a repricing that added a December hike and opened the door to 50bp. Positioning explains it: equities had already fallen four straight weeks.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Major — modest equity moves, but a wholesale repricing of the global rate path.
**Breadth:** Systemic — equities, bonds, FX and precious metals all repriced; Asia FX followed.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — ratifies the hiking inversion begun by the prior week's +165K payrolls.
**Calculated Score:** (7 × 3.0) × 1.25 = **26.25**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
Rate-sensitive growth and semis absorbed the hit; Consumer Staples and Energy outperformed. Fed pricing moved to ~88.8% for 25bp on Dec 9 (from ~72%), with 50bp odds rising from zero to 13.6%, later 14.5%. A Reuters poll had all 101 economists expecting 25bp. Officials turned uniformly hawkish — Logan backs two more hikes, Williams called inflation "far too high," Hammack urged a restrictive stance, Schmid said inflation is undefeated; only Goolsbee dissented in tone, calling the report "distressing." Waller had pre-committed to considering 50bp if core printed ≥0.3% — it did.

### 2. Jensen Huang Resigns as Nvidia CEO (Impact Score: 22.50)

**Event Date:** 8-K accepted Nov 11; announced Thursday, Nov 12, 2026
**Event Type:** Corporate / Governance
**News Source:** NVIDIA newsroom + SEC Form 8-K (official); CNBC, Axios, PBS (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
Nvidia's board announced Huang had resigned as President, CEO and director, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO while retaining the CFO role; Harvey C. Jones was named the company's first board chair. No reason was given. Huang had admitted to extramarital affairs, and reporting tied the departure to a board investigation involving a woman allegedly connected to smuggling advanced AI chips into China — **reported, not confirmed**; China denies it. The Senate signalled the resignation does not end its national-security inquiry.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate:** NVDA fell as much as 13% intraday before partially rebounding, closing the week −12.75%. Friday saw the follow-through: SOX −3.4% (worst day since Nov 5), AMD −9%, Technology the only sector down more than 1%, S&P 500 −0.40%.
**Follow-Through:** Reversed at the sector level, sustained at the stock level — a divergence, not a recovery.
**Pattern Comparison:** **Inverse at the sector level.** The reference pattern for a mega-cap shock is sector-wide contagion dragging the index. Instead the SOX *gained 8.05%* on the week (SOXX +9.35%), led by AMD +19% and Broadcom +18%. Investors treated a governance failure at Nvidia as a transfer of franchise value to its competitors, not as an indictment of AI demand.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — a −13% intraday move in the index's largest weight.
**Breadth:** Sector-Wide — semis and the AI complex repriced; rates and FX did not respond.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change — a leadership vacuum at the most systemically important stock in the market, under an unresolved national-security investigation, five days before earnings.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 1.5) × 1.50 = **22.50**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
A rotation within AI rather than out of it. Nvidia reports Q3 FY2027 on Nov 17 after the close against prior guidance of $108bn ±2% (consensus ~$109bn) and 74% gross margin, with management already flagging a 71–72% margin trough in the January quarter on rising memory costs — now to be defended by an interim CEO.

### 3. Dollar Hits 19-Month High; Yen Breaches 160 (Impact Score: 15.00)

**Event Date:** Thursday–Friday, Nov 12–13, 2026
**Event Type:** FX / Monetary Policy
**News Source:** Reuters (Tier 1); Japan MoF statements (official)

#### Event Summary
The dollar index closed at 103.41, its highest since April 2025, driven by the widening Fed–rest-of-world policy gap. USD/JPY briefly breached 160 on Thursday before settling at 159.875. Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama said the government would "respond appropriately" to volatility — the standard verbal precursor to intervention, and a line Japan has already backed with action twice in 2026. The BoJ remains at 1.25% after holding on Oct 30, leaving the yen a funding currency.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate:** DXY +0.23% Friday to a 19-month high; Asia FX held losses; gold and silver were liquidated.
**Follow-Through:** Sustained, with two-way risk — the rate gap pulls the dollar up while intervention threat can erase a week's move in one session.
**Pattern Comparison:** **Consistent and amplified** — this was the channel through which the CPI shock reached commodities.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Moderate — a level and threshold story, not a magnitude story; daily moves were small.
**Breadth:** Systemic — DXY, JPY, Asia FX, precious metals, and Japanese policy reaction function.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation.
**Calculated Score:** (4 × 3.0) × 1.25 = **15.00**

### 4. IEA Flags First Post-War Oil Surplus Against Houthi Strikes (Impact Score: 12.00)

**Event Date:** Monday–Friday, Nov 9–13, 2026
**Event Type:** Commodity / Geopolitical
**News Source:** IEA (official); Reuters, CNBC (Tier 1); Al Jazeera

#### Event Summary
The IEA's Nov 13 report put Q4 supply at 105mb/d — 1.7mb/d above demand and the first surplus since the Iran war began — with October oversupply near 2mb/d and a 3.8mb/d surplus projected for 2027. Against this, the Houthis claimed strikes on the Yanbu facility, an East-West Pipeline pumping station and an Aramco building in Riyadh on Nov 12, following a Nov 9 pipeline attack that sparked a fire without halting operations. The pipeline matters disproportionately: it is Saudi Arabia's main route bypassing Iran's Strait of Hormuz blockade. Separately, Trump said on Nov 12 that the US "could do without" an Iran deal and would keep the blockade; Iran refused to surrender "a single atom" of enriched material.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate:** Brent settled $95.84 (−2.52%, −$2.48) and WTI $88.56 (−2.50%, −$2.27) — a second consecutive weekly decline after the prior week's 16%/13% collapse. Thursday's attacks lifted Brent $3.06 and WTI $2.21 intraday.
**Pattern Comparison:** **Severely dampened.** The 2019 Aramco attacks moved oil +15% in a single day; comparable infrastructure strikes this week produced roughly +3%, fully surrendered by Friday. A 1.7mb/d surplus is now absorbing geopolitical risk premium that would have been explosive twelve months ago.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Moderate — −2.5% weekly with a +3% intraweek geopolitical spike.
**Breadth:** Cross-Asset — energy, energy equities, inflation expectations.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change — "first surplus since the war began" marks the end of scarcity pricing.
**Calculated Score:** (4 × 2.0) × 1.50 = **12.00**

#### Commodity Correlation
Oil fell on OPEC+ raising October output, rising US crude and fuel stocks, and the blockade's paradoxical effect of keeping Iranian barrels off-market while the rest of the world oversupplies. Note the dislocation: the Energy *sector* led all S&P sectors at +5.1% while crude fell 2.5% — equity investors bid Saudi supply risk and the prior week's oversold condition, not the spot price.

### 5. October PPI Hits 6.5% YoY — Fastest Since 2022 (Impact Score: 10.00)

**Event Date:** Friday, Nov 13, 2026, 08:30 ET
**Event Type:** Economic Data
**News Source:** BLS (official); CNBC, Yahoo Finance (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
Final-demand PPI rose 0.3% m/m — *below* the 0.4% consensus and a sharp deceleration from September's 1.4% — but the annual rate climbed to 6.5% from 6.3%, the fastest since late 2022 and more than double last year's 3.1%. Core rose 0.3% m/m (~4.9–5.0% YoY). Wholesale food jumped 1.3%, the most since July 2022, while energy slipped 0.1% and diesel fell 7.9%. This was the last CPI or PPI release before the Dec 9 decision.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate:** Muted and mixed. Gold steadied near $3,885 (+0.18%) as the monthly undershoot failed to cool hike bets; December pricing held fully. Friday's equity weakness was Nvidia-driven, not PPI-driven.
**Pattern Comparison:** **Dampened** — the m/m undershoot neutralised the headline annual number; markets traded the margin, not the level.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Moderate | **Breadth:** Cross-Asset | **Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation
**Calculated Score:** (4 × 2.0) × 1.25 = **10.00**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
Broadening pipeline pressure — food-led, no longer energy-led — is the harder problem for the Fed, since it cannot be dismissed as a commodity passthrough. Diesel's 7.9% drop confirms the energy disinflation visible in crude.

## Thematic Synthesis

### Dominant Market Narrative
Equities rebounded hard through a hawkish shock. The market has stopped treating a tightening Fed as an existential threat and started discriminating — bidding cyclicals and AI alternatives while liquidating the assets that depend on falling real rates.

### Interconnected Events
Events 1, 3 and 5 are one chain: the CPI beat repriced the Fed path, which lifted the dollar to a 19-month high, which crushed gold and silver; PPI then ratified it. Event 2 ran orthogonally — Nvidia's governance shock hit the index's largest weight on Thursday and Friday without touching rates or FX. Event 4 cut against Event 1's inflation impulse: a 1.7mb/d oil surplus is actively disinflationary and smothered a geopolitical supply shock that historically moves crude 15%.

### Market Regime Assessment
**Risk Appetite:** Risk-On — rate-shock tolerant
**Evidence:** VIX −24.73% to 19.30 (Wednesday low close 17.49); S&P +2.51% snapping a four-week losing streak; Energy +5.1% and Industrials +3.6% leading with Materials (−0.4%) the only declining sector; breadth positive (≈315 advancers). Gold and silver fell not on complacency but on real-rate repricing.
**Sector Rotation:** Cyclicals over defensives; within technology, a violent rotation from Nvidia (−12.75%) to AMD (+19%) and Broadcom (+18%), leaving SOX +8.05% on the week.

### Commodity Deep Dive
- **Energy:** WTI $88.56 (−2.50%), Brent $95.84 (−2.52%); second weekly decline on the IEA surplus, higher OPEC+ output and rising US stocks.
- **Precious Metals:** Gold lost $4,000, closing $3,884.69 after a $185.90 (−4.57%) weekly drop — its worst since early June. Silver fell 6.10% to $49.93 spot, with COMEX futures settling $48.65, a one-year low. Dollar strength and hike repricing overrode both the inflation hedge and an active Middle East supply threat.
- **Base Metals:** Materials was the only S&P sector to decline (−0.4%), consistent with dollar strength.

### Anomalies and Surprises
1. **Semis rose 8% while Nvidia fell 13%.** A governance collapse at the AI trade's centre produced rotation, not contagion — the single most important signal of the week.
2. **Gold fell 4.6% during live attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure.** The geopolitical premium framework inverted completely; real rates dominated.
3. **Energy equities led (+5.1%) while crude fell 2.5%.**
4. **Infrastructure strikes moved oil ~3%, versus +15% for the 2019 Aramco precedent.** Surplus has repriced geopolitical risk.

## Forward-Looking Implications

### Market Positioning Insights
The four-week downtrend broke, but leadership has narrowed and changed hands. With the S&P up 10.5% YTD and a December hike fully priced, the asymmetry now sits in the 50bp tail — unpriced a week ago, ~14.5% now. Precious metals are the cleanest expression of the hawkish path; a hot PCE would extend the liquidation.

### Risk Scenarios
1. **50bp December hike (~15%, Severe):** Waller's precondition has been met. Would hit long-duration equities and gold hardest.
2. **Nvidia earnings miss or guidance cut under interim leadership (~30%, Severe):** Margin trough already flagged at 71–72%; no permanent CEO to defend the capex narrative.
3. **Japanese FX intervention (~35%, Major):** USD/JPY above 160 with explicit MoF warnings; would reverse the dollar trade violently in one session.
4. **Iran escalation closing the East-West Pipeline (~15%, Major):** The surplus is absorbing strikes now, but the bypass route to Hormuz is the one genuine chokepoint left.
5. **Nvidia national-security inquiry widens (~25%, Moderate):** Senate has signalled the resignation does not close the matter.
