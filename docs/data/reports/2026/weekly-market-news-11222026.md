# Weekly Market News — 11/22/2026

## Executive Summary

Analysis period 11/16–11/22/2026 (trading week Nov 16–20); five Tier-1 events identified. The AI trade broke. The S&P 500 fell **8.6% to 6,910.39** — its worst week since April 2025 — closing a seventh consecutive down day, the first such streak since February 2020, and entering correction 11.6% below its record and 6.0% beneath a 200-day average it sliced through at 7,349. The Nasdaq Composite lost **10% to ~22,618**, its worst week since March 2020, surrendering its entire 2026 gain; the Nasdaq-100 (−10.43% to 25,028.98) finished 19.84% off its high, **49 points from a bear market**. Semiconductors were the epicentre: the SOX fell **17.13% to 8,408.59**, worse than April 2025 (−16.0%) or March 2020 (−15.9%) and its deepest week since September 2001. The proximate trigger was Friday's Reuters report that SEC enforcement staff are examining Nvidia's accounting — NVDA −14% to $125.53, roughly $491bn of value gone in a session. Critically, this was a de-rating, not a growth scare: Nvidia had *beaten* on Nov 17 with $115.8bn revenue and guided Q4 to ~$130bn, and Anthropic's record Nasdaq debut on Nov 19 opened +34% before fading to +17% and then −8.5%. The haven bid went to the dollar (DXY ~103.84, fourth straight weekly gain) and Treasuries (10Y −22bp to 4.932%) — not to gold, which fell another **9.1% to ~$3,531**, its worst week in eight months, as hawkish October FOMC minutes locked a December 9 hike at ~92–96%.

## Detailed Event Analysis

### 1. Reuters: SEC Examining Nvidia's Accounting (Impact Score: 45.00)

**Event Date:** Friday, Nov 20, 2026, intraday
**Event Type:** Corporate / Regulatory
**News Source:** Reuters (Tier 1, two people briefed); SEC declined comment

#### Event Summary
Reuters reported that SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues at Nvidia, without establishing when the inquiry began or its precise scope. Nvidia did not immediately respond. Coverage noted renewed scrutiny of the company's prior change in how it reports Singapore revenue, though Reuters did not confirm that this is part of the inquiry. An investigation is not evidence of wrongdoing and may close without action. The timing was the problem: it landed three days after a clean beat, into a tape already destabilised by Jensen Huang's Nov 12 resignation, an open board investigation, and an unresolved permanent CEO search.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate (Day-of):** Equities — NVDA −14.0% to $125.53, a 52-week low and its worst session since the DeepSeek shock of January 2025, erasing ~$491bn; the SOX −10.7% with roughly $1.3trn of chip market cap destroyed in one session (Dow Jones Market Data). The S&P 500 fell 3.7%, the Nasdaq-100 5.61% (−1,488 points), the Nasdaq Composite ~5.3%. Breadth collapsed: decliners led advancers 376 to 123, Technology −7.27% and Consumer Staples the sole gainer at +0.67%. Rates — flight to quality: 10Y −12bp to 4.932%, 2Y to ~4.63–4.65%, a bull steepening led by the front end. FX — dollar bid as a haven. Crypto — Bitcoin to $61.8K. Volatility — VIX to ~36, spiking above 39 intraday from a 28.44 Thursday close.
**Follow-Through:** None within the week; Friday was the seventh straight decline and the week's low close.
**Pattern Comparison:** **Amplified.** A headline-risk regulatory story on a single name does not normally move the S&P 500 3.7%. The amplifier was concentration: with ~70% of Nasdaq-100 members already 20%+ below their highs and 20 of 22 semiconductor and memory names in bear markets, the probe supplied a catalyst for positioning that was already mechanically fragile.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — index moves >2%, SOX −17.13% on the week, NVDA −14% in a day.
**Breadth:** Systemic — equities, credit, Treasuries, FX and crypto all repriced simultaneously.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change — governance and disclosure risk is now priced into the AI complex's largest name, independent of its fundamentals.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 3.0) × 1.50 = **45.00**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
The credit channel is the one to watch. CoreWeave's five-year CDS widened toward **1,233bp** on Nov 20 — above the 1,000bp distress threshold — with its 9.625% 2032 bond yielding north of 16%. Ten-year hyperscaler debt has widened ~50bp year-to-date against ~20bp for non-AI high-grade industrials, and banks that were competing to fund AI deals weeks ago are now signalling reluctance even where marquee tech guarantees are attached. A BlackRock high-yield tracker fell 0.8% on the week, its sharpest in over a month. Nvidia is itself now a financier of its customers: JPMorgan flagged that its promised payments and guarantees nearly doubled in a single quarter. Equity weakness is becoming a funding problem, which is the mechanism by which an AI de-rating stops being contained to equities.

### 2. Hawkish October FOMC Minutes Lock In December (Impact Score: 26.25)

**Event Date:** Wednesday, Nov 18, 2026, 14:00 ET
**Event Type:** Monetary Policy
**News Source:** FederalReserve.gov (official); FXStreet, Reuters (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
Minutes of the Oct 27–28 meeting showed almost all participants judging that additional tightening would be needed under their baseline outlooks, having held the range at 3.75–4.00% on a 10–2 vote with Cook and Hammack dissenting in favour of a hike. A couple of participants said increases exceeding a traditional 25bp could be warranted if inflation fails to cool. Several noted price pressures were increasingly extending beyond industries directly tied to data-centre construction — the committee's clearest statement yet that the AI capex impulse has leaked into the general price level. Speakers reinforced it: Philadelphia's Paulson flagged inflation risks warranting another increase, Cook publicly defended her dissent on Nov 20, while Williams offered the only counterweight, saying the situation does not call for dramatic action.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate (Day-of):** The dollar rallied; gold and tech fell. December 9 pricing ended the week at roughly 92% (CNBC/CME FedWatch Friday) to 95.7% (FedWatch, Nov 19), with Kalshi near 90%; one gold desk cited 99.3%. A 50bp move remains a 3–10% tail.
**Follow-Through:** Partly reversed in rates — the 10Y *fell* 22bp on the week despite hike certainty rising, as equity-driven haven demand overwhelmed the policy signal. The dollar channel held: DXY closed ~103.84, a fourth consecutive weekly gain and its highest since April 2025.
**Pattern Comparison:** **Dampened in rates, consistent in FX and metals.** Sources disagreed on whether the minutes exceeded expectations; the honest read is that the hawkish message was mostly priced, and the week's rate move was equity risk, not policy.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Major — a 22bp weekly move in the 10Y and a 9% move in gold, against a modest DXY gain.
**Breadth:** Systemic — rates, FX, metals and equity multiples all repriced.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — ratifies the hiking inversion established by October CPI.
**Calculated Score:** (7 × 3.0) × 1.25 = **26.25**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
The Fed is now tightening into an equity correction and visible credit stress — the defining tension for December 9. Rate-sensitive consumer sectors are already transmitting it: Home Depot flagged mortgage rates near 7.1% as a headwind alongside weakening customer traffic.

### 3. Gold Falls Another 9.1%; the Haven Correlation Breaks (Impact Score: 25.00)

**Event Date:** Nov 16–20, 2026
**Event Type:** Commodity / Monetary Policy
**News Source:** CNBC, Kitco, Reuters (Tier 1); USAGOLD (specialised)

#### Event Summary
Spot gold fell to a Friday low of **$3,513.54**, its weakest since Sep 4, 2025, before closing the week near $3,531–3,544 — down roughly 8.7–9.1% from $3,885.60, the worst week since the week ended March 20. That is a second consecutive brutal week after the prior week's −4.57%. Silver fell alongside it. The driver was rate expectations and the dollar, not risk appetite: bullion sold off hardest as policymakers queued up to flag inflation risks.

#### Market Reaction
**Pattern Comparison:** **Inverse.** Gold is supposed to rally in an 8.6% equity drawdown. It fell 9%. The mechanism is a liquidity squeeze layered on a real-rate shock — when investors need cash and the dollar is the designated haven, bullion is a source of funds rather than a destination. Physical demand in India and China strengthened on the dip, which is what a price-driven rather than fear-driven decline looks like.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — a 9% weekly move is triple the severe threshold.
**Breadth:** Cross-Asset — precious metals and FX.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — a second consecutive week of real-rate-led liquidation.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 2.0) × 1.25 = **25.00**

#### Commodity Correlation
Gold's failure as a haven is the week's cleanest diagnostic signal: this correction is an AI-valuation and funding event, not a macro fear event. Investors who needed protection bought dollars and 2-year notes. If gold begins rising while equities fall, the character of the selloff has changed — that is the tell to watch.

### 4. Oil's Third Straight Weekly Decline on Hormuz Normalisation (Impact Score: 18.75)

**Event Date:** Nov 16–20, 2026
**Event Type:** Commodity / Geopolitical
**News Source:** Reuters via CNBC (Tier 1); EnergyNow (specialised)

#### Event Summary
December WTI settled at **$83.98**, down $4.58 or **5.17%** on the week; January Brent fell $3.27 or **3.41%** to $92.57, widening the Brent-WTI premium to $8.59. Both benchmarks fell for a third consecutive week as crude shipments through the Strait of Hormuz returned to roughly pre-war levels, hardening expectations of a supply surplus. Friday saw a partial rebound on renewed Middle East tensions. Note that December WTI expired Nov 20, so intraday quotes circulating at 6% and 8% weekly declines reflect contract roll rather than settlements.

#### Market Reaction
**Pattern Comparison:** **Consistent.** Restored supply through a chokepoint reliably compresses the geopolitical risk premium; the surprise is the speed of normalisation, not the direction. Energy equities held up relatively well, down only 0.62% on Friday against Technology's −7.27%.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — WTI −5.17% exceeds the 5% threshold.
**Breadth:** Sector-Wide — the energy complex and energy equities.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — a third week of the post-war surplus thesis building.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 1.5) × 1.25 = **18.75**

#### Commodity Correlation
Falling crude into a hawkish Fed is the one genuinely helpful cross-current: it relieves headline inflation pressure precisely as the committee debates whether 25bp is enough. Sustained sub-$85 WTI would weaken the case for the 50bp tail.

**Event Date:** Thursday, Nov 19, 2026
**Event Type:** Corporate / IPO
**News Source:** Fortune, Forbes, Fast Company (Tier 1/2)

#### Event Summary
Anthropic priced at **$83**, above its $70–78 indicated range, and listed on Nasdaq as ANT. Shares opened at **$111.50** (+34%), reached $115.10, traded as low as $92.72, and closed at **$97.17**, up about 17%. Reported proceeds vary across sources ($81.3bn to $107.3bn) and should be treated as unreconciled pending filings. Instead the pop more than halved into the close and the Nasdaq fell 5.3% the next day. A record debut that fails to lift its own sector is strong evidence of exhausted marginal demand for AI exposure at current multiples.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Major — a 17-point round trip from the open and −8.5% on day two.
**Breadth:** Sector-Wide — the AI complex and the IPO pipeline.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — confirms the de-rating thesis.
**Calculated Score:** (7 × 1.5) × 1.25 = **13.13**

## Thematic Synthesis

### Dominant Market Narrative
The AI trade de-rated on valuation, governance and funding — not on fundamentals. Nvidia delivered $115.8bn of revenue with data centre up 110% year-on-year to $107.6bn, adjusted EPS of $2.68 against $2.47 expected, and guided Q4 to ~$130bn; the stock still ended the week near $125, roughly 40% off its peak and $2.7trn lighter than October. When a sixth consecutive beat cannot hold a bid, the market is repricing the multiple, not the earnings. Evercore, Truist and Bernstein cut to Hold after the print.

### Interconnected Events
Three strands compounded. (1) Hawkish Fed minutes raised the discount rate applied to long-duration growth. (2) The Anthropic fade removed the last sentiment support. (3) The SEC headline supplied the catalyst into mechanically fragile positioning. Offsetting: falling crude and a 22bp rally in the 10Y are genuine easing impulses that cushioned the drawdown.

### Market Regime Assessment
**Risk Appetite:** Risk-Off.
**Evidence:** All eleven SPDR sectors lower on the week — Technology −12.49%, Communication Services −7.97%, Materials −7.45%, against Staples −2.05%, Utilities −3.07% and Real Estate −4.30%. VIX ~36 (>39 intraday). Treasuries bid, dollar at a 19-month high, high-yield funds down 0.8%, Bitcoin at $61.8K.
**Sector Rotation:** A textbook defensive rotation — out of growth and semiconductors, into staples and utilities — with the unusual feature that gold was sold, not bought.

### Commodity Deep Dive
- **Energy:** WTI $83.98 (−5.17%), Brent $92.57 (−3.41%); third weekly loss on Hormuz normalisation and surplus expectations.
- **Precious Metals:** Gold ~$3,531 (−9.1%), low $3,513.54; silver lower. Real-rate and dollar-driven, with physical dip-buying in Asia.
- **Base Metals / FX:** DXY ~103.84, fourth weekly gain, highest since April 2025. USD/JPY retreated from 160 to ~158.86 on risk aversion, with intervention risk live; markets price a BOJ hike to 1.50% on Dec 17–18 (86% of economists polled by Reuters) after Japanese CPI hit its highest since November 2025.

### Anomalies and Surprises
1. **Gold −9% during an 8.6% equity correction.** The single most informative anomaly of the week.
2. **A beat-and-raise that could not hold.** Nvidia rose 2–4% after hours on Nov 17 and ended the week at a 52-week low.
3. **Yields fell 22bp as hike odds rose to ~95%.** Equity risk overwhelmed policy in the rates market.
4. **Consumer resilience ignored.** Home Depot beat with $43.4bn sales, $3.86 adjusted EPS and +2.2% comps; Walmart raised full-year guidance to $2.84–2.89 adjusted EPS. Neither registered.

## Forward-Looking Implications

### Market Positioning Insights
The S&P 500 is 6.0% below a broken 200-day average at 7,349 and the Nasdaq-100 sits 49 points from a formal bear market — a threshold likely to be tested on any further weakness. With roughly 70% of Nasdaq-100 members already 20%+ off their highs, the index-level damage understates the internal damage; a seventh straight down day into a holiday week with thin liquidity cuts both ways and argues for outsized moves in either direction.

### Upcoming Catalysts
- **October core PCE — Wed Nov 25** (preview: +0.3% m/m, 3.2% y/y from 3.1%; headline 0.3% m/m, 3.8% y/y). The key December input; a hot print moves the debate toward 50bp.
- **Pre-holiday data block — Tue Nov 24 to Wed Nov 25:** consumer confidence, Case-Shiller, durable goods, personal income and spending, new home sales, jobless claims. Fed Beige Book Thursday; Williams speaking. Markets closed Thu Nov 26; early 13:00 ET close Fri Nov 27.
- **FOMC decision — Wed Dec 9.** ~92–96% priced for 25bp.
- **Bank of Japan — Dec 17–18.** Hike to 1.50% expected.
- **Dell earnings — Tue Nov 24,** the next read on AI server demand.

### Risk Scenarios
1. **Nasdaq-100 confirms a bear market (45%).** Impact: Major. A 49-point gap in thin holiday trading is a low bar; confirmation would trigger systematic de-risking and CTA selling.
2. **AI credit contagion (30%).** Impact: Severe. CoreWeave CDS at 1,233bp, bank funding reluctance and Nvidia's doubling vendor guarantees are the transmission path from equity de-rating to a genuine funding event.
3. **Hot core PCE forces the 50bp debate (25%).** Impact: Severe. A Fed delivering 50bp into a correction with widening spreads is the tail the market is least positioned for.
4. **Nvidia probe escalates (20%).** Impact: Major. Any confirmation of scope, a formal order or a restatement risk would re-rate the entire AI supply chain's disclosure quality.
5. **Policy-error reversal (20%).** Impact: Major, upside. If December is framed as the final hike amid visible financial stress, the de-rating reverses violently from deeply oversold levels.
