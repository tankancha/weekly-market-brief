# Weekly Market News — 11/22/2026

## Executive Summary

Analysis period 11/16–11/22/2026 (trading week Nov 16–20); five Tier-1 events identified. The AI trade cracked. The S&P 500 fell **8.61% to 6,910.39** — its worst week since April 2025 — on a seventh consecutive down day, the longest losing run since February 2020, entering correction 11.6% below its record and slicing through a 200-day average at 7,349. The Nasdaq Composite lost **10.02% to 22,617.73**, its worst week since March 2020, turning negative for 2026; the Nasdaq-100 (−10.43% to 25,028.98) finished **49.41 points** from a bear market after trading through the line intraday. The Dow fell 8.02% to 48,147.33, Russell 2000 −7.48%, and the VIX nearly doubled to **35.89**. Semiconductors were the epicentre: the SOX fell **17.13% to 8,408.59**, worse than April 2025 (−16.0%) or March 2020 (−15.9%) and its deepest week since September 2001. Nvidia lost **23%** — its worst week since October 2008 — closing Friday at $125.53 after Reuters reported SEC enforcement staff are examining its accounting. Critically, this was a de-rating, not a growth scare: Nvidia had *beaten* on Nov 17 ($115.8bn revenue, +103% y/y, Q4 guided to $130bn), October retail sales beat, and Target posted its best sales growth in five years. The liquidation was indiscriminate across metals — gold −9%, silver up to −15.4%, copper −10% — while the haven bid went to the dollar (DXY 103.84, a 19-month high) and Treasuries (2Y −20bp, 10Y back below 5%).

## Detailed Event Analysis

### 1. Reuters: SEC Examining Nvidia's Accounting (Impact Score: 45.00)

**Event Date:** Friday, Nov 20, 2026, intraday
**Event Type:** Corporate / Regulatory
**News Source:** Reuters (Tier 1, two people briefed); earlier WSJ report Nov 17

#### Event Summary
The *Wall Street Journal* disclosed on Nov 17 that a federal probe of Nvidia existed, without identifying its focus. On Friday Nov 20, Reuters reported — citing two people briefed on the matter — that **SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues**, while unable to establish when the inquiry began or its scope. The SEC declined comment; Nvidia did not immediately respond. Coverage linked scrutiny to a change Nvidia made last year in how it reports revenue by geography, though Reuters did not say this is part of the inquiry. An investigation is not evidence of wrongdoing. The timing was the problem: it landed three days after a clean beat, into a tape already destabilised by Jensen Huang's Nov 12 resignation and an unresolved CEO search.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate (Day-of):** NVDA was already down ~7% before the story broke and closed **−14.01% at $125.53**, a 52-week low and roughly **$500bn of value in one session**; it fell in 9 of the last 10 sessions and −23% on the week, its worst since October 2008. The SOX fell 10.7% with ~$1.3trn of chip market cap destroyed (Dow Jones Market Data). AMD lost 20.5% on the week to $424.39. The S&P 500 fell 3.70%, the Nasdaq-100 5.61% (−1,488.07 points), the Nasdaq Composite 5.28%. Breadth collapsed — decliners over advancers **376 to 123**, Technology −7.27% and Consumer Staples the sole gainer (+0.67%); SPY saw an **$11.65bn weekly outflow**, the largest of any ETF. **Rates:** flight to quality — 2Y −20bp to 4.66%, the steepest weekly fall since August 2025; 10Y to ~4.93–4.95%, back below 5%. **FX:** dollar bid as a haven. **Crypto:** Bitcoin to $61.8K. **Volatility:** VIX 35.89, +86% on the week, above 39 intraday.
**Follow-Through:** None; Friday was the seventh straight decline and the week's low close.
**Pattern Comparison:** **Amplified.** A single-name regulatory headline does not normally move the S&P 500 3.70%. The amplifier was concentration and prior fragility: ~70% of Nasdaq-100 members were already 20%+ below their highs and 20 of 22 semiconductor and memory names were in bear markets. Thursday Nov 19 had already seen Meta −12.60%, Tesla −11.38%, Alphabet −10.31%, Microsoft −7.51% and Amazon −7.44% in a single session — the probe supplied a catalyst for a de-rating already underway.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — index moves >2%, SOX −17.13%, NVDA −14% in a day.
**Breadth:** Systemic — equities, credit, Treasuries, FX and crypto repriced simultaneously.
**Forward Significance:** Regime Change — governance and disclosure risk is now priced into the AI complex's largest name, independent of its fundamentals.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 3.0) × 1.50 = **45.00**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
The credit channel is the one to watch, and one strategist's framing captures it: "a rates event in credit clothing." CoreWeave's five-year CDS sat at **1,100–1,200bp** — above the 1,000bp distress threshold — against Q3 capex of $14.5bn and a $132.5bn backlog. Ten-year hyperscaler spreads have widened ~50bp year-to-date versus ~20bp for non-AI industrials; weekly default-swap volume on AI names is approaching $10bn, and chip CDS has for the first time in three months surpassed cloud vendors. Goldman Sachs expects further widening into early December (IG above ~93bp, HY above ~335bp), and CNBC reported Nov 18 that ETF issuers are preparing products to let investors bet against AI debt. Nvidia is itself a financier of its customers: JPMorgan flagged that its promised payments and guarantees nearly doubled in a quarter. Equity weakness is becoming a funding problem — the mechanism by which an AI de-rating stops being contained to equities.

### 2. Hawkish FOMC Minutes and Waller's "Shock Therapy" (Impact Score: 26.25)

**Event Date:** Wednesday, Nov 18, 2026, 14:00 ET
**Event Type:** Monetary Policy
**News Source:** FederalReserve.gov (official); CNBC, Reuters, Newsquawk (Tier 1)

#### Event Summary
Minutes of the Oct 27–28 meeting — which held the range at 3.75–4.00% with **Governor Cook and Cleveland's Hammack dissenting in favour of a 25bp hike** — showed "almost all" participants seeing further hikes needed and "a couple" open to a move larger than 25bp, with almost all judging inflation risks tilted to the upside. Several noted price pressures were increasingly extending beyond industries tied to data-centre construction: the committee's clearest statement yet that the AI capex impulse has leaked into the general price level. Hours earlier, **Governor Waller told CNBC that a 50bp December hike should be considered — "shock therapy."** Cook separately signalled hikes could extend beyond December and argued rates remain too accommodative; Philadelphia's Paulson flagged inflation risks warranting another increase. Williams was the lone counterweight, saying the situation does not call for dramatic action. Trump's public call for rate cuts was ignored.

#### Market Reaction
**Immediate (Day-of):** The dollar rallied against the euro and sterling; gold fell. **CME-implied odds of a 50bp December move jumped from ~3.1% to ~10%** on Waller's remarks (one source: 12.5%). December 25bp pricing ended the week in a **~89–97% range** across CME FedWatch readings and prediction markets.
**Follow-Through:** Reversed in rates, held in FX. The 10-year *fell* ~19–22bp on the week despite hike certainty rising, as equity-driven haven demand overwhelmed the policy signal; the 2s10s steepened marginally to ~+30bp. The dollar channel held: DXY closed 103.84 after touching a 19-month high of **103.97** Thursday, a fourth consecutive weekly gain and its strongest since early April 2025.
**Pattern Comparison:** **Dampened in rates, consistent in FX and metals.** The hawkish message was largely priced; the week's rate move was equity risk, not policy.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Major — a ~20bp weekly move across the curve and a 9% move in gold, against a modest DXY gain.
**Breadth:** Systemic — rates, FX, metals and equity multiples all repriced.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — ratifies the hiking inversion established by October CPI.
**Calculated Score:** (7 × 3.0) × 1.25 = **26.25**

#### Sector-Specific Impacts
The Fed is now tightening into an equity correction with visible credit stress — the defining tension for December 9. Rate-sensitive housing is already transmitting it: Nov 18 housing starts beat at 1,309,000, but single-family permits fell 8.7% m/m and NAHB builder sentiment held at 29, a 14-year low, with CNBC flagging "a bleak winter ahead." Home Depot cited mortgage rates near 7.1% as a headwind.

### 3. The Metals Complex Is Liquidated (Impact Score: 25.00)

**Event Date:** Nov 16–20, 2026
**Event Type:** Commodity / Monetary Policy
**News Source:** CNBC, Kitco, Reuters (Tier 1); Ausecure, goldprice.com, Longbridge (specialised)

#### Event Summary
**Gold** fell roughly **9%** to ~$3,520–3,540 from $3,884.69, a fifth consecutive decline and a 12-to-14-month low, touching $3,513.54 intraday — its worst week in eight months and a second brutal week after the prior week's −4.57%. **Silver** fell harder, to ~$41.2, down **13% to 15.4%** depending on source: its worst week of 2026. **Copper** lost about **10%**, its largest weekly drop since August 1. Platinum and palladium also fell more than gold.

#### Market Reaction
**Pattern Comparison:** **Inverse — and the mechanisms differ, which is the analytically important part.** Gold and silver fell on real rates, dollar strength and forced liquidation: a cash-raising squeeze layered on a hawkish Fed, with physical demand in India and China strengthening on the dip — what a price-driven rather than fear-driven decline looks like. **Copper fell for an entirely different reason:** analysts attributed it explicitly to doubt about the sustainability of AI spending, not Chinese demand. Copper traded as an AI-capex proxy. That two unrelated metals channels both broke in the same week is the cleanest evidence that this was a cross-asset de-leveraging, not a macro fear event.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — gold −9%, silver to −15.4%, copper −10%; all multiples of the severe threshold.
**Breadth:** Cross-Asset — precious metals, base metals and FX.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — a second consecutive week of real-rate-led liquidation.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 2.0) × 1.25 = **25.00**

#### Commodity Correlation
Gold's failure as a haven in an 8.6% equity drawdown is the week's cleanest diagnostic signal: investors who needed protection bought dollars and 2-year notes. If gold begins rising while equities fall, the character of the selloff has changed — that is the tell to watch.

### 4. Oil's Third Straight Weekly Decline (Impact Score: 18.75)

**Event Date:** Nov 16–20, 2026
**Event Type:** Commodity / Geopolitical
**News Source:** Reuters via CNBC (Tier 1); EIA (official); EnergyNow (specialised)

#### Event Summary
December WTI settled at **$83.98**, down $4.58 or **5.17%** on the week; January Brent fell $3.27 or **3.41%** to $92.57, widening the Brent-WTI premium to $8.59. Two forces drove it: crude shipments through the **Strait of Hormuz returned to roughly pre-war levels**, and the EIA reported on Nov 18 a **second consecutive build** — commercial crude +3.38m bbl to 417.80m, well above expectations. Bullish news was insufficient: Houthi attacks on Riyadh, Bahrain intercepting Iranian attacks, the US extending its blockade of Iranian shipping to ship-to-ship transfers on Nov 18, and Iran preparing 14 tankers to run the blockade produced only a Friday bounce. Trump called new strikes "quite unlikely," suggesting the next move may not come until 2027. Note December WTI expired Nov 20, so intraday quotes circulating at 6–8% weekly declines reflect contract roll, not settlements.

#### Market Reaction
**Pattern Comparison:** **Consistent.** Restored supply through a chokepoint reliably compresses the geopolitical risk premium; the surprise is the speed of normalisation while hostilities remain active. Energy equities held up relatively well, down only 0.62% on Friday against Technology's −7.27%.

#### Impact Assessment Detail
**Price Impact:** Severe — WTI −5.17% exceeds the 5% threshold.
**Breadth:** Sector-Wide — the energy complex and energy equities.
**Forward Significance:** Trend Confirmation — a third week of the post-war surplus thesis building.
**Calculated Score:** (10 × 1.5) × 1.25 = **18.75**

#### Commodity Correlation
Falling crude into a hawkish Fed is the one genuinely helpful cross-current: it relieves headline inflation pressure precisely as the committee debates whether 25bp is enough. Sustained sub-$85 WTI would weaken the case for the 50bp tail. Natural gas was quieter — the EIA reported a +20 Bcf injection on Nov 19 to 3,882 Bcf, smaller than expected but still 80 Bcf above the five-year average.

A record debut that cannot lift its own sector is strong evidence of exhausted marginal demand for AI exposure at current multiples.

## Thematic Synthesis

### Dominant Market Narrative
The AI trade de-rated on valuation, governance and funding — not on fundamentals. Nvidia delivered revenue of **$115.8bn (+103% y/y)** against ~$108.7bn consensus, data centre **$107.6bn (+110%)**, gross margin 74.8%, non-GAAP EPS **$2.68** versus $2.47 expected, and guided Q4 to **$130.0bn ±3%**, while returning $28.1bn in the quarter. The stock lost 23%. When a sixth consecutive beat of that magnitude cannot hold a bid, the market is repricing the multiple, not the earnings — and Evercore, Truist and Bernstein cut to Hold after the print. The arithmetic behind the doubt is now visible: the five largest hyperscalers spent **$237.3bn in the latest quarter** against $121.9bn a year earlier, tracking $803–828bn for CY2026, and over the first nine months of 2026 their combined capex (~$556.9bn) **exceeded operating cash flow (~$542.9bn)**. Free cash flow has inverted, which is why the equity de-rating is showing up in credit.

### Interconnected Events
Four strands compounded. (1) Hawkish minutes plus Waller's 50bp trial balloon raised the discount rate on long-duration growth. (2) The Anthropic fade removed the last sentiment support and added record equity supply. (3) The SEC headline supplied the catalyst into mechanically fragile positioning. (4) Copper's 10% fall confirmed the de-rating had reached the real-economy proxy for AI capex. Offsetting: falling crude and a ~20bp rally across the curve are genuine easing impulses that cushioned the drawdown. The week's major geopolitical event was, unusually, a non-event — Trump and Xi met on the APEC sidelines in Shenzhen Nov 18–19, alongside a first-ever Trump–Xi–Putin trilateral, and the **US–China trade truce was left in place** through its Jan 10, 2027 expiry, with chip export controls explicitly off the table. Preserved status quo, no market impact.

### Market Regime Assessment
**Risk Appetite:** Risk-Off.
**Evidence:** All eleven SPDR sectors lower — Technology −12.49%, Communication Services −7.97%, Materials −7.45%, Real Estate −4.30%, against Utilities −3.07% and Staples −2.05%. VIX 35.89 (+86%, above 39 intraday). Treasuries bid across the curve, dollar at a 19-month high, high-yield funds −0.8%, Bitcoin at $61.8K, SPY outflows of $11.65bn.
**Sector Rotation:** A textbook defensive rotation out of growth and semiconductors into staples and utilities — with the unusual feature that gold and silver were sold, not bought.

### Commodity Deep Dive
- **Energy:** WTI $83.98 (−5.17%), Brent $92.57 (−3.41%); third weekly loss on Hormuz normalisation and a second EIA build. Nat gas +20 Bcf injection to 3,882 Bcf.
- **Precious Metals:** Gold ~$3,530 (−9%, 12–14-month low), silver ~$41.2 (−13% to −15.4%, worst week of 2026). Real-rate and liquidity-driven, with Asian physical dip-buying.
- **Base Metals:** Copper ~−10%, worst week since August 1 — traded explicitly as an AI-capex proxy rather than a China-demand story.
- **FX:** DXY 103.84, fourth weekly gain, Thursday high 103.97, strongest since early April 2025. USD/JPY ~159.1–159.7, testing 160 for a third time in six sessions; Vice Minister Mimura warned coordinated US–Japan intervention remains possible, with MUFG expecting action between 160 and 164. Markets price a BOJ hike to 1.50% on Dec 17–18 (86% of economists polled by Reuters) after Japanese CPI hit its highest since November 2025.

### Anomalies and Surprises
1. **Gold −9% and silver −15% during an 8.6% equity correction.** The most informative anomaly of the week.
2. **A beat-and-raise that could not hold.** Nvidia rose 2–4% after hours on Nov 17 and ended the week down 23% at a 52-week low.
3. **Yields fell ~20bp as hike odds rose toward 95%.** Equity risk overwhelmed policy in the rates market.
4. **A strong consumer tape, selectively ignored.** October retail sales beat (+0.2% m/m, +6.8% y/y; control group +0.6% vs +0.4%). Target posted comps **+5.6%** versus +3.8% expected — its best sales growth in over five years — sales $27.1bn (+7.1%), adjusted EPS $2.65 versus $2.07, and *raised* FY guidance to $10.65–11.40; shares rose ~7%. Walmart beat on $188.8bn revenue (+5.2%) with eCommerce +17% and raised FY adjusted EPS to $2.84–2.89. But Home Depot's beat ($43.4bn sales, $3.86 adjusted EPS) came with a narrowed FY outlook (~$14.98 vs $15.01 consensus) and a CEO succession — CFO Richard McPhail steps up — and shares fell ~2%; Lowe's beat with comps +1.8% and raised sales guidance yet fell ~4% as the market discounted a $0.34/share tariff refund. The consumer was fine; only clean, refund-free beats were paid for.

## Forward-Looking Implications

### Market Positioning Insights
The S&P 500 is 6.0% below a broken 200-day average at 7,349 and the Nasdaq-100 sits **49.41 points** from a formal bear market — a threshold likely to be tested on any further weakness. With ~70% of Nasdaq-100 members already 20%+ off their highs, index-level damage understates internal damage. A seventh straight down day into a holiday week with thin liquidity cuts both ways and argues for outsized moves in either direction.

### Upcoming Catalysts
- **Flash PMIs — Mon Nov 23** (US, eurozone, Germany, UK).
- **October core PCE — Wed Nov 25, 08:30 ET** (headline +0.3% m/m expected, easing from +0.5%; core +0.3%), with the **Q3 GDP second estimate** (+3.5% expected, from +3.4%) and both EIA reports pulled forward. The single most important print before December.
- **Holiday liquidity:** markets closed Thu Nov 26; early 13:00 ET close Fri Nov 27.
- **OPEC+ — Sun Nov 29.** Sets 2027 quotas with 2.0 mb/d of cuts in place; a live catalyst after three down weeks in crude.
- **FOMC — Dec 8–9, decision Dec 9.** ~89–97% priced for 25bp, ~10–12.5% for 50bp.
- **Broadcom (Dec 9) and Oracle (~Dec 14)** — the next high-stakes reads on AI demand and AI credit.
- **G20, Doral — Dec 14–15** (possible second Trump–Xi meeting); **BOJ Dec 17–18** (hike to 1.50% expected); **US–China truce expiry Jan 10, 2027.**

### Risk Scenarios
1. **Nasdaq-100 confirms a bear market (45%).** Impact: Major. A 49-point gap in thin holiday trading is a low bar; confirmation would trigger systematic de-risking and CTA selling.
2. **AI credit contagion (30%).** Impact: Severe. CoreWeave CDS at 1,100–1,200bp, inverted hyperscaler free cash flow, bank funding reluctance and Nvidia's doubling vendor guarantees are the transmission path from equity de-rating to a funding event. Goldman already expects IG past 93bp and HY past 335bp into early December.
3. **Hot core PCE forces the 50bp debate (25%).** Impact: Severe. Waller has pre-committed to the argument; a Fed delivering 50bp into a correction with widening spreads is the tail the market is least positioned for.
4. **Nvidia probe escalates (20%).** Impact: Major. Confirmation of scope, a formal order or restatement risk would re-rate the entire AI supply chain's disclosure quality.
5. **Policy-error reversal (20%).** Impact: Major, upside. If December is framed as the final hike amid visible financial stress — and with a demonstrably solid consumer — the de-rating reverses violently from deeply oversold levels.
